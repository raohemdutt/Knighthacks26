# Wavefront-level levers for the student's LoRA training step on gfx1151: where a 44 s step can go, and what each lever should buy

Prediction note for the kernels track, written for Task Board task R42 on 2026-10-11, before any GPU time is spent on it (Ilan's rule: no slot without a prediction note). It explains the first real training run of the student on the Strix Halo (L16 on pod D, node pod-b: Qwen3.5-4B in bf16, PEFT LoRA r 16 on every language-model linear, batch 4 x accumulation 4, 18 optimizer steps in 785 s), says where that time can be going and which measurement tells each candidate apart, and then, kernel class by kernel class, names the wavefront-level levers on RDNA 3.5 (wave32 against wave64, occupancy against VGPR and LDS use, block and tile sizes, `num_warps` and `num_stages` for Triton, launch-count reduction) with the predicted effect from the R33 chip sheet's arithmetic and the run that confirms or kills it, one lever per run. The microbenchmark harness that measures them is `lab/kernels/wavefront/` (README section "Wavefront microbenchmarks"). Inference, the vision tower and the training recipe are out of scope; the step-level levers (fla bound, checkpointing off, micro-batch 16) are R37's (`predictions/train_step_gfx1151.md`, PR 308) and are referred to, not repeated.

**This is a prediction ahead of the profile.** R34, the training-step profile (PR 326, branch `ctx/runtime/train-profile-gfx1151`), has not run on a pod; every share of a step below is a prediction for R34's numbers to replace.

## Summary

The arithmetic floor of everything the step must compute or move is under 1 s per micro-batch of 4 examples; L16 spent about 12.5 s per micro-batch. No bandwidth or FLOP account closes a gap of more than 10x, and neither does a plausible kernel-launch count, so the note predicts that most of the 44 s step is in one of three places that are not the GEMMs: one-time costs counted inside a short 18-step run, a few pathologically slow kernels on the torch fallbacks (the depthwise causal conv and the float32 Gated DeltaNet reference), or host-side stalls. Each has a signature R34 reads directly (section 2). The wavefront-level levers matter only after the step is near its floor: on today's step the best of them (LDS-aware `num_stages` for the fla kernels, split-K for the LoRA adapters) are each predicted under 2 percent of a step.

## 1. The step to explain

- **[measured]** L16 trained the student on pod D (node pod-b) with the L3 trainer's pinned baseline: LoRA r 16, per-device batch 4 x gradient accumulation 4, 18 optimizer steps in 785 s (about 43.6 s a step), 0.32 examples per second, peak 11.1 GiB of GPU memory. Source: the R42 task page, dispatcher line of Sun 2026-10-11 restating L16's run result ([R42 task page](https://app.notion.com/p/3f596ef5fec58145a787e23c8101dd92)); `lab/train.py::BASELINE` for the settings (main at 69aa5b4).
  - Implication: 785 s x 0.32 examples per second is about 251 examples, not 18 x 16 = 288, so not every step held 16 examples; our inference is that the run was 84 training rows (L12's frozen train side) for 3 epochs, 21 micro-batches of 4 per epoch, 6 optimizer steps per epoch with a short last one, which gives 18 steps, 63 micro-batches and 252 examples. On that reading a micro-batch of 4 examples took about 12.5 s (785 / 63) and an example about 3.1 s. R37 predicted 0.40 examples per second (0.25 to 0.60) for this step, so L16 lands inside R37's band and below its central value.
  - Candidate experiment: WF0, read the per-step wall times the run already logged (the Hugging Face trainer's step log, or `trainer.samples_per_s` against step count) and R34's 20 profiled steps; no slot. Expected movement: none in samples per second; it splits the 785 s into one-time and steady-state time. Minimum sample size: 1 run (all 18 steps). Confidence: medium (0.55).

- **[measured]** On the same pod the chip sheet measured bf16 GEMMs of a 4B LoRA step at 1,024 tokens and K = N = 2560 at 42.3 TFLOP/s forward and 42.7 TFLOP/s input gradient; the rank-16 adapter GEMMs at 1.58 (A forward, 0.053 ms), 4.50 (B forward, 0.019 ms), 2.47 (A weight gradient, 0.034 ms) and 2.03 TFLOP/s (B weight gradient, 0.041 ms); device copies at 209.5 to 212.1 GB/s from 256 MiB up and 576.8 to 663.5 GB/s at 1 to 2 MiB (cache-resident). Source: [L10] `lab/refine/research/kernels/data/chipsheet-gfx1151-2026-10-11.json`, sections "lora" and "copy" (15 repeats each).
  - Implication: per trained token the frozen base costs about 24 GFLOP (forward, checkpoint recompute and input gradient; R37 section 3), so at 42.3 TFLOP/s a 256-token example has a GEMM floor of about 0.145 s and a micro-batch of 4 about 0.58 s; L16's 12.5 s per micro-batch is about 21x that floor at 256 tokens, and still about 5x at 1,024 tokens. The row lengths of L12's data are not recorded anywhere we opened, so the floor is quoted at both lengths (Open questions).
  - Candidate experiment: none beyond WF0 and R34's measurement of achieved matmul rate during the step. Expected movement: none; R34 should find GEMM time 4 to 20 percent of a step. Minimum sample size: 5 profiled steps. Confidence: medium (0.55).

- **[measured]** The chip sheet's occupancy curve, a latency-bound pointer chase on `CUs x 32` workgroups of 256 threads, holds 1.95 to 1.99 Gload/s from 4 to 192 live VGPRs and from 0 to 65,536 bytes of LDS per workgroup, then falls to 1.12 Gload/s (0.56x) at 240 live VGPRs; rocminfo reports 40 compute units and the HIP runtime 20 (`multiProcessorCount`), wavefront size 32, 65,536 bytes of LDS per workgroup. Source: [L10] chip sheet, sections "Occupancy curve", "Device" and "LDS".
  - Implication: the LLVM occupancy table for gfx1151 (16 waves per SIMD up to 96 VGPRs, 8 at 192, 6 at 240; `strix_halo_hardware.md` section 2, [S81]) says a latency-bound kernel on this chip keeps its rate down to about 8 waves per SIMD and loses about half of it at 6; so the VGPR budget a training kernel can spend before it costs time is about 192 per lane, twice the 96 that keeps full occupancy. HIP's 20 is the WGP count (40 CUs in WGP mode), which is the number a grid must exceed to fill the chip (20 workgroups minimum in WGP mode).
  - Candidate experiment: WF4 reads the same curve from the other side: one Triton tile kernel compiled at several `num_warps` and `num_stages`, its `n_regs`, `n_spills` and shared bytes from Triton's metadata turned into waves per SIMD with the same table, beside its measured time (section 6). Expected movement: none in samples per second; time flat until the compiled kernel drops below 8 waves per SIMD. Minimum sample size: 5 runs per point. Confidence: medium (0.5).

## 2. Where 44 s can go, and the signature that tells each candidate apart

- **[opinion]** Adding every byte and FLOP floor of a micro-batch of 4 x 256 tokens from the chip sheet gives under 1 s: base GEMMs 0.58 s (section 1), the Gated DeltaNet reference's float32 activations 0.15 to 0.23 s (R37's 32 to 48 GB at 209.5 GB/s), the depthwise conv 0.015 s (section 4), the loss over 248,320 vocabulary entries about 0.02 s (four passes over 1 GB of float32 logits), the LoRA adapters 0.07 s (section 5). L16's 12.5 s per micro-batch leaves more than 11 s that no floor accounts for. Source: arithmetic on [L10] (`lora.bf16.*`, `copy.d2d.size_1gib`), [S121] `modeling_qwen3_5.py::torch_chunk_gated_delta_rule` and `Qwen3_5GatedDeltaNet.__init__` at 536ecc0 (shapes), and the R37 note `predictions/train_step_gfx1151.md` sections 2 to 4 on branch `ctx/runtime/predictions-gfx1151` at 1e2c4b4.
  - Implication: tuning a kernel toward its floor cannot explain or remove the gap until the gap is found; the first runs are diagnostic (WF0 to WF3), and the wavefront-level levers (WF4, WF5 and the tile choices of sections 3 to 5) are ordered after them.
  - Candidate experiment: R34's profile (20 steps, `torch.profiler` with shapes, rocprofv3 kernel trace when present). Expected movement: none in samples per second; GPU-busy time (the sum of kernel durations over wall time) under 50 percent of a step, which points at host time or one-time costs, or one kernel family over 30 percent, which points at a pathological kernel. Minimum sample size: 5 profiled steps after 5 warm-up steps. Confidence: medium (0.5).

- **[opinion]** Candidate A, one-time costs inside a short run: an 18-step run pays, inside its timed steps, for lazily loaded GEMM code objects, MIOpen's first call per convolution shape (and the trainer pads per micro-batch, so each new padded length is a new shape), and, if fla is ever bound, Triton compilation of every autotune config of every kernel on a cold cache (fla v0.5.2's `solve_tril` alone lists 16 configs, `num_warps` 1, 2, 4, 8 by `num_stages` 2 to 5). 120 s of such cost would add about 6.7 s to the mean of 18 steps. Signature: the first steps (and the first micro-batch of each new padded length) far slower than the median of the rest. Source: [S190] fla v0.5.2 `fla/ops/utils/solve_tril.py` autotune lists; `lab/train.py` docstring (padding per micro-batch, no packing, main at 69aa5b4).
  - Implication: if WF0 shows it, the fair baseline for every lever is the median step after warm-up, never 785 / 18, and the 20 to 70 percent of the mean it removes is not a kernel gain; R37's T1 to T3 deltas must be read against the same median.
  - Candidate experiment: WF0, as specified in section 1; for MIOpen, the harness's `conv` class records the first call per shape apart from the steady repeats (WF2). Expected movement: steps 3 onward 20 to 70 percent below the 43.6 s mean. Minimum sample size: 1 run. Confidence: medium (0.55).

- **[opinion]** Candidate B, a few pathologically slow kernels: the torch Gated DeltaNet reference runs float32 (no WMMA path for float32 on gfx1151; WMMA covers f16, bf16, iu8 and iu4 only), two batched `torch.linalg.solve_triangular` calls per layer, and a Python loop over 64-token chunks, under autograd and twice under checkpointing; the causal conv runs as a depthwise `nn.Conv1d` with 8,192 groups and kernel 4, a shape a convolution library may serve with a generic kernel. Signature: one kernel family (by name: `trsm` or `solve`, `MIOpen` or `naive` conv, small float32 `Cijk` GEMMs) over 30 percent of GPU-busy time in R34's top 25. Source: [S121] `torch_chunk_gated_delta_rule` (float32 casts, `solve_triangular`, `for i in range(num_chunks)`) and `Qwen3_5GatedDeltaNet.__init__` (`nn.Conv1d(groups=self.conv_dim, kernel_size=self.conv_kernel_size)`, `conv_dim = key_dim * 2 + value_dim` = 2 x 2048 + 4096) at 536ecc0; `strix_halo_hardware.md` section 6 ([S80], [S82]) for the WMMA types.
  - Implication: this is the candidate the wavefront work can act on directly, by replacing the kernel (fla's Triton chunk kernels, fla's Triton conv) and then tuning the replacement's tiles for 64 KiB of LDS and wave32 (sections 3 and 4).
  - Candidate experiment: WF1 and WF2 (sections 3 and 4) time each family alone at the step's shapes. Expected movement: none in samples per second by themselves; they size each family per layer so that 24 layers x 4 micro-batches can be set against the 44 s step. Minimum sample size: 5 repeats per case. Confidence: medium (0.5).

- **[opinion]** Candidate C, host-side cost: the step launches tens of thousands of small kernels per micro-batch in eager PyTorch (our estimate: about 600 per Gated DeltaNet layer per micro-batch over forward, recompute and backward, times 24 layers, plus about 16 per LoRA-wrapped linear times 248, about 18,000 in all), each paying the framework's dispatch and the HIP launch, and any host-device synchronization (an `.item()`, a host copy, a CPU fallback op) stalls the queue. At 10 us per op that is about 0.2 s of a 12.5 s micro-batch (1.5 percent); to explain the whole gap each op would have to cost about 600 us. Signature: GPU-busy under 50 percent of a step with short kernels and long gaps between them, and host-device copies or `hipDeviceSynchronize` calls inside the step. Source: arithmetic on [S121]'s chunk loop and [S190]-style kernel counts; HIP's documentation that launch cost is negligible through the HIP API but can dominate behind framework layers (HIP docs, "HIP graphs", [S192]).
  - Implication: launch count alone is predicted to be a small share today (1 to 10 percent), so launch-count reduction (HIP graphs, fusing the LoRA adapter pair, torch.compile once fla is bound) ranks after A and B; a host stall, if R34 finds one, is a one-line fix and ranks first.
  - Candidate experiment: WF3, the harness's `launch` class: per-op wall time of a chain of tiny kernels launched eagerly against the same chain replayed as one HIP graph (`torch.cuda.CUDAGraph`), on pod D. Expected movement: none in samples per second by itself; eager 5 to 20 us per op, graph 1 to 3 us per op; multiplied by R34's kernel count it gives the launch share of a step. Minimum sample size: 5 repeats of 2,000-op chains. Confidence: medium (0.6).
