# Wavefront-level levers for the student's LoRA training step on gfx1151: where a 44 s step can go, and what each lever should buy

Prediction note for the kernels track, written for Task Board task R42 on 2026-10-11, before any GPU time is spent on it (Ilan's rule: no slot without a prediction note). It explains the first real training run of the student on the Strix Halo (L16 on pod D, node pod-b: Qwen3.5-4B in bf16, PEFT LoRA r 16 on every language-model linear, batch 4 x accumulation 4, 18 optimizer steps in 785 s), says where that time can be going and which measurement tells each candidate apart, and then, kernel class by kernel class, names the wavefront-level levers on RDNA 3.5 (wave32 against wave64, occupancy against VGPR and LDS use, block and tile sizes, `num_warps` and `num_stages` for Triton, launch-count reduction) with the predicted effect from the R33 chip sheet's arithmetic and the run that confirms or kills it, one lever per run. The microbenchmark harness that measures them is `lab/kernels/wavefront/` (README section "Wavefront microbenchmarks"). Inference, the vision tower and the training recipe are out of scope; the step-level levers (fla bound, checkpointing off, micro-batch 16) are R37's (`predictions/train_step_gfx1151.md`, PR 308) and are referred to, not repeated.

**This is a prediction ahead of the profile.** R34's training-step profiler is on main (`lab/profile_train.py`, PR 326) and has not run on a pod; every share of a step below is a prediction for R34's numbers to replace. One run is no longer a prediction: WF0, the free one, was read from L16's committed per-step telemetry on 2026-10-11, after the predictions were written. Its result is the last finding of section 1; the predictions it tests are left as they were written.

## Summary

The arithmetic floor of everything the step must compute or move is under 1 s per micro-batch of 4 examples; L16 spent about 12.5 s per micro-batch. No bandwidth or FLOP account closes a gap of more than 10x, and neither does a plausible kernel-launch count, so the note predicts that most of the 44 s step is in one of three places that are not the GEMMs: one-time costs counted inside a short 18-step run, a few pathologically slow kernels on the torch fallbacks (the depthwise causal conv and the float32 Gated DeltaNet reference), or host-side stalls. Each has a signature R34 reads directly (section 2). The wavefront-level levers matter only after the step is near its floor: on today's step the best of them (LDS-aware `num_stages` for the fla kernels, split-K for the LoRA adapters) are each predicted under 2 percent of a step.

WF0's result (section 1) kills WF0 as it was written and corrects two numbers of the paragraph above. The median of steps 3 to 18 is 40.3 s, 7.7 percent below 785 / 18, so the first steps are not where a fifth of the run went. The one-time cost is real and sits in two other places: 78 s of `trainer.train()` lie outside every step, and the first epoch's five full steps averaged 56.4 s against 40.3 s for the ten of epochs 2 and 3, 80 s more; together 158 s, 20 percent of the 785 s. The baseline for every lever is therefore the steady full step, 40.3 s, which is 10.1 s per micro-batch and not 12.5 s. And the rows are 577 to 911 tokens long, 694 on average, not 256: the floors scale to about 2.3 to 2.5 s per micro-batch, so the part no floor explains is about 7.6 to 7.8 s of each micro-batch, a gap of about 4x and not of more than 10x. The three candidates and the order of the runs stand.

- **[measured]** L16 trained the student on pod D (node pod-b) with the L3 trainer's pinned baseline: LoRA r 16, per-device batch 4 x gradient accumulation 4, 18 optimizer steps in 785 s (about 43.6 s a step), 0.32 examples per second, peak 11.1 GiB of GPU memory. Source: [L24] `fixtures/demo/l16-run.json` (the trainer's own answer: 18 steps, 784.5 s of `trainer.train()`, peak 11.1 GiB) and `lab/L16_CONFIG.md` (84 rows, 252 examples trained), which replace the dispatcher's restatement on the R42 task page that this finding was first written from; `lab/train.py::BASELINE` for the settings (main at 69aa5b4).
  - Implication: 785 s x 0.32 examples per second is about 251 examples, not 18 x 16 = 288, so not every step held 16 examples; we inferred, and `lab/L16_CONFIG.md` now confirms, that the run was 84 training rows for 3 epochs, 21 micro-batches of 4 per epoch, 6 optimizer steps per epoch with a short last one, which gives 18 steps, 63 micro-batches and 252 examples. Over the whole 785 s a micro-batch of 4 examples took about 12.5 s (785 / 63) and an example about 3.1 s; the steady values are lower (last finding of this section). R37 predicted 0.40 examples per second (0.25 to 0.60) for this step, so L16 lands inside R37's band and below its central value.
  - Candidate experiment: WF0, read the per-step wall times the run already logged (the Hugging Face trainer's step log, or `trainer.samples_per_s` against step count) and R34's 20 profiled steps; no slot. Expected movement: none in samples per second; it splits the 785 s into one-time and steady-state time. Minimum sample size: 1 run (all 18 steps). Confidence: medium (0.55).

The row lengths of L12's data are not recorded anywhere we opened, so the floor is quoted at both lengths (Open questions).
  - Candidate experiment: none beyond WF0 and R34's measurement of achieved matmul rate during the step. Expected movement: none; R34 should find GEMM time 4 to 20 percent of a step. Minimum sample size: 5 profiled steps. Confidence: medium (0.55).

Expected movement: none in samples per second; time flat until the compiled kernel drops below 8 waves per SIMD. Minimum sample size: 5 runs per point. Confidence: medium (0.5).

Minimum sample size: 1 run of 12 optimizer steps. Confidence: medium (0.5). ## 2. Where 44 s can go, and the signature that tells each candidate apart

- **[opinion]** Adding every byte and FLOP floor of a micro-batch of 4 x 256 tokens from the chip sheet gives under 1 s: base GEMMs 0.58 s (section 1), the Gated DeltaNet reference's float32 activations 0.15 to 0.23 s (R37's 32 to 48 GB at 209.5 GB/s), the depthwise conv 0.015 s (section 4), the loss over 248,320 vocabulary entries about 0.02 s (four passes over 1 GB of float32 logits), the LoRA adapters 0.07 s (section 5). L16's 12.5 s per micro-batch leaves more than 11 s that no floor accounts for. (At the rows' real 694 tokens and the steady 10.1 s micro-batch WF0 found, the same floors come to about 2.3 to 2.5 s and the part they leave to about 7.6 to 7.8 s: section 1.) Source: arithmetic on [L10] (`lora.bf16.*`, `copy.d2d.size_1gib`), [S121] `modeling_qwen3_5.py::torch_chunk_gated_delta_rule` and `Qwen3_5GatedDeltaNet.__init__` at 536ecc0 (shapes), and the R37 note `predictions/train_step_gfx1151.md` sections 2 to 4 (on main since PR 308; read on branch `ctx/runtime/predictions-gfx1151` at 1e2c4b4).
  - Implication: tuning a kernel toward its floor cannot explain or remove the gap until the gap is found; the first runs are diagnostic (WF0 to WF3), and the wavefront-level levers (WF4, WF5 and the tile choices of sections 3 to 5) are ordered after them.
  - Candidate experiment: R34's profile (20 steps, `torch.profiler` with shapes, rocprofv3 kernel trace when present). Expected movement: none in samples per second; GPU-busy time (the sum of kernel durations over wall time) under 50 percent of a step, which points at host time or one-time costs, or one kernel family over 30 percent, which points at a pathological kernel. Minimum sample size: 5 profiled steps after 5 warm-up steps. Confidence: medium (0.5).

- **[opinion]** Candidate A, one-time costs inside a short run: an 18-step run pays, inside its timed steps, for lazily loaded GEMM code objects, MIOpen's first call per convolution shape (and the trainer pads per micro-batch, so each new padded length is a new shape), and, if fla is ever bound, Triton compilation of every autotune config of every kernel on a cold cache (fla v0.5.2's `solve_tril` alone lists 16 configs, `num_warps` 1, 2, 4, 8 by `num_stages` 2 to 5). 120 s of such cost would add about 6.7 s to the mean of 18 steps. Signature: the first steps (and the first micro-batch of each new padded length) far slower than the median of the rest. Source: [S183] fla v0.5.2 `fla/ops/utils/solve_tril.py` autotune lists; `lab/train.py` docstring (padding per micro-batch, no packing, main at 69aa5b4).
  - Implication: if WF0 shows it, the fair baseline for every lever is the median step after warm-up, never 785 / 18, and the 20 to 70 percent of the mean it removes is not a kernel gain; R37's T1 to T3 deltas must be read against the same median. WF0 has since found one-time cost of another shape (section 1), and the baseline it gives is the steady full step of epochs 2 and 3, 40.3 s.
  - Candidate experiment: WF0, as specified in section 1; for MIOpen, the harness's `conv` class records the first call per shape apart from the steady repeats (WF2). Expected movement: steps 3 onward 20 to 70 percent below the 43.6 s mean. Minimum sample size: 1 run. Confidence: medium (0.55).

- **[opinion]** Candidate B, a few pathologically slow kernels: the torch Gated DeltaNet reference runs float32 (no WMMA path for float32 on gfx1151; WMMA covers f16, bf16, iu8 and iu4 only), two batched `torch.linalg.solve_triangular` calls per layer, and a Python loop over 64-token chunks, under autograd and twice under checkpointing; the causal conv runs as a depthwise `nn.Conv1d` with 8,192 groups and kernel 4, a shape a convolution library may serve with a generic kernel. Signature: one kernel family (by name: `trsm` or `solve`, `MIOpen` or `naive` conv, small float32 `Cijk` GEMMs) over 30 percent of GPU-busy time in R34's top 25. Source: [S121] `torch_chunk_gated_delta_rule` (float32 casts, `solve_triangular`, `for i in range(num_chunks)`) and `Qwen3_5GatedDeltaNet.__init__` (`nn.Conv1d(groups=self.conv_dim, kernel_size=self.conv_kernel_size)`, `conv_dim = key_dim * 2 + value_dim` = 2 x 2048 + 4096) at 536ecc0; `strix_halo_hardware.md` section 6 ([S80], [S82]) for the WMMA types.
  - Implication: this is the candidate the wavefront work can act on directly, by replacing the kernel (fla's Triton chunk kernels, fla's Triton conv) and then tuning the replacement's tiles for 64 KiB of LDS and wave32 (sections 3 and 4).
  - Candidate experiment: WF1 and WF2 (sections 3 and 4) time each family alone at the step's shapes. Expected movement: none in samples per second by themselves; they size each family per layer so that 24 layers x 4 micro-batches can be set against the 44 s step. Minimum sample size: 5 repeats per case. Confidence: medium (0.5).

- **[opinion]** Candidate C, host-side cost: the step launches tens of thousands of small kernels per micro-batch in eager PyTorch (our estimate: about 600 per Gated DeltaNet layer per micro-batch over forward, recompute and backward, times 24 layers, plus about 16 per LoRA-wrapped linear times 248, about 18,000 in all), each paying the framework's dispatch and the HIP launch, and any host-device synchronization (an `.item()`, a host copy, a CPU fallback op) stalls the queue. At 10 us per op that is about 0.2 s of a 12.5 s micro-batch (1.5 percent); to explain the whole gap each op would have to cost about 600 us. Signature: GPU-busy under 50 percent of a step with short kernels and long gaps between them, and host-device copies or `hipDeviceSynchronize` calls inside the step. Source: arithmetic on [S121]'s chunk loop and [S183]-style kernel counts; HIP's documentation that launch cost is negligible through the HIP API but can dominate behind framework layers (HIP docs, "HIP graphs", [S185]).
  - Implication: launch count alone is predicted to be a small share today (1 to 10 percent), so launch-count reduction (HIP graphs, fusing the LoRA adapter pair, torch.compile once fla is bound) ranks after A and B; a host stall, if R34 finds one, is a one-line fix and ranks first.
  - Candidate experiment: WF3, the harness's `launch` class: per-op wall time of a chain of tiny kernels launched eagerly against the same chain replayed as one HIP graph (`torch.cuda.CUDAGraph`), on pod D. Expected movement: none in samples per second by itself; eager 5 to 20 us per op, graph 1 to 3 us per op; multiplied by R34's kernel count it gives the launch share of a step. Minimum sample size: 5 repeats of 2,000-op chains. Confidence: medium (0.6).

## 3. Kernel class 1: the Gated DeltaNet chunk kernels

- **[practitioner]** flash-linear-attention v0.5.2 sizes its Gated DeltaNet tiles by the device's shared memory: `check_shared_mem(arch)` compares Triton's `max_shared_mem` with 101,376 bytes ("ada"), 166,912 ("ampere"), 232,448 ("hopper") or 102,400 (the default), and a part below them gets the small lists: the forward state kernel (`chunk_delta_h.py`) autotunes `BV` over [32] instead of [32, 64] and `num_stages` over [2, 1] instead of [2, 3, 4], with `num_warps` [2, 4]; `chunk_o.py` takes `BK = BV = 32`; `cumsum.py` block sizes [16, 32]; `wy_fast.py` a constant tiling of 32; on AMD (`IS_AMD`, Triton backend `hip`) the Triton causal conv autotunes `num_warps` over [2, 4, 8, 16] instead of [4, 8, 16, 32]; the triangular solve (`solve_tril.py`) autotunes `num_warps` 1, 2, 4, 8 by `num_stages` 2 to 5 on every part. Source: [S183] fla v0.5.2 (9c8e42e, 2026-07-28), `fla/utils/_device.py::Backend` and `check_shared_mem`, `fla/ops/common/chunk_delta_h.py`, `fla/ops/common/chunk_o.py`, `fla/ops/utils/cumsum.py`, `fla/ops/gated_delta_rule/wy_fast.py`, `fla/ops/utils/solve_tril.py`, `fla/modules/conv/triton/kernels.py`.
  - Implication: gfx1151 reports 65,536 bytes per workgroup (chip sheet `lds.hip.shared_per_block_bytes`), below every threshold, so fla already restricts itself to the small, LDS-safe tiles here; the wavefront levers left inside fla are `num_warps` and `num_stages` within its lists, and the cost of compiling every listed config on a cold cache (Candidate A). The NVIDIA-tuned lists were not written for this part, but the size check moves them in the right direction.
  - Candidate experiment: WF1, the harness's `gdn` class: one Gated DeltaNet layer's chunk rule at the step's shapes (batch 4, 256 and 1,024 tokens, 16 key heads and 32 value heads of 128, chunk 64), forward plus backward, lever `impl` = `torch` (transformers' `torch_chunk_gated_delta_rule`) against `fla` (`fla.ops.gated_delta_rule.chunk_gated_delta_rule`, skipped and recorded as skipped when fla is not importable), the first call timed apart from the repeats. Expected movement: reference 40 to 200 ms per layer per micro-batch, fla 5x to 20x faster after its first call; 24 layers x 4 micro-batches x the difference is the step time the binding removes. Minimum sample size: 5 repeats per case after 2 warm-up calls. Confidence: medium (0.5).

- **[practitioner]** Triton 3.5.1 compiles every kernel on gfx10 and later as wave32 (`warp_size = 32 if gfx_major >= 10 else 64` in `HIPOptions.__post_init__`), defaults `num_warps` to 4, `waves_per_eu` to 1 and `num_stages` to 2, writes `amdgpu-flat-work-group-size` as 1 to `num_warps x warp_size` and `amdgpu-waves-per-eu` from `waves_per_eu`, and hands `num_stages` to its AMD stream pipeliner; the compiled kernel exposes `n_regs` and `n_spills` from the loaded binary. Source: [S184] triton-lang/triton v3.5.1 (0add682), `third_party/amd/backend/compiler.py::HIPOptions` and `make_llir`, `python/triton/compiler/compiler.py::CompiledKernel._init_handles`.
  - Implication: in Triton there is no wave64 lever on this chip (wave64 exists only for HIP code built with `-mwavefrontsize64`, as the chip sheet's probe was); `num_warps = 4` is 128 lanes here, the same thread count it is on NVIDIA where fla's lists were tuned, unlike on CDNA's wave64 where it is 256. So "kernels compiled at their defaults for another vendor's wave size" costs nothing in block size on RDNA; the risk moves to per-lane register tiling (VGPRs per lane, read from `n_regs`) and to the LDS each pipeline stage takes, which the next finding sizes.
  - Candidate experiment: WF4, as specified in the next finding. Expected movement: see there. Minimum sample size: 5 repeats per config. Confidence: low (0.4).

- **[opinion]** For fla's forward state kernel on gfx1151 at `BV = 32`: the float32 state tile is K 128 x BV 32 = 16 KiB per workgroup, 32 VGPRs per lane across 128 lanes at `num_warps` 4, so the kernel should compile to 64 to 120 VGPRs (12 to 16 waves per SIMD by the occupancy table), well above the chip sheet's knee of 8; the grid is batch 4 x 32 value heads x 4 V tiles = 512 workgroups over 20 WGPs. The LDS decides the rest: at `num_stages` 2 the pipeliner keeps two copies of a 64 x 128 bf16 key tile (16 KiB) and a 64 x 32 bf16 value tile (4 KiB), about 40 KiB per workgroup, so 3 workgroups (12 waves) fit in a WGP's 128 KiB; at `num_stages` 1 about 20 KiB, 6 workgroups (24 waves). The scan over chunks is sequential inside a workgroup and latency-bound, which is the case where resident waves matter, so stages 1 is predicted at least as fast as stages 2 here (within 10 percent or faster), the opposite of the order of fla's NVIDIA list. Source: arithmetic on [S183] (`BV`, chunk size 64, `num_stages` lists), [S121] `configuration_qwen3_5.py` (linear-attention key and value head dimension 128, 16 key and 32 value heads), [S80] and [S81] through `strix_halo_hardware.md` sections 2 and 3 (1,536 VGPRs, granule 24, 128 KiB LDS per WGP), and [L10] (the occupancy curve's knee).
  - Implication: when fla is bound, pinning `num_stages` 1 for the scan kernels removes half of their autotune configs (shorter cold start) at no predicted cost, and the tile and stage choice can be checked from Triton's metadata without a profiler.
  - Candidate experiment: WF4, the harness's `tile` class: a standalone Triton kernel with the shape of fla's scan step (a 64 x 128 by 128 x 32 `tl.dot` per chunk, a float32 state carried across 4 to 16 chunks), lever `num_stages` 1 against 2 at `num_warps` 4 (one lever per run; `num_warps` 2, 4, 8 is the next run), each row carrying `n_regs`, `n_spills`, shared bytes and the waves per SIMD and workgroups per WGP they imply. Expected movement: stages 1 within 10 percent of stages 2 or faster; waves per SIMD from `n_regs` within the table. Minimum sample size: 5 repeats per config. Confidence: low (0.4). Kill criterion: stages 2 faster by more than 10 percent at both sequence lengths.

## 4. Kernel class 2: the depthwise causal conv

- **[practitioner]** In transformers the Gated DeltaNet layer holds its short convolution as `nn.Conv1d(in_channels=conv_dim, out_channels=conv_dim, groups=conv_dim, kernel_size=linear_conv_kernel_dim, padding=kernel - 1)` with `conv_dim = key_dim * 2 + value_dim`, and passes its weight to `causal_conv1d_fn`, which is the `causal-conv1d` package when installed and a torch reference otherwise; the stock pod image has neither `causal-conv1d` nor fla, and fla v0.5.2 ships its own Triton causal conv. Source: [S121] `Qwen3_5GatedDeltaNet.__init__` and `forward` at 536ecc0; `settings_training_gfx1151.md` section 6 ([S122], [S123]); [S183] `fla/modules/conv/triton/kernels.py`.
  - Implication: with 2 x 2048 + 4096 = 8,192 channels and kernel 4, each of the 24 layers runs a depthwise conv forward, again under recompute, and its two gradients; a depthwise conv of this shape is all memory traffic, so its cost is the kernel the library picks for it, which is a wavefront-level question (one channel per lane, a short reduction, no LDS needed).
  - Candidate experiment: WF2, the harness's `conv` class, as specified in the next finding. Expected movement: see there. Minimum sample size: 5 repeats per case. Confidence: low (0.4).

- **[opinion]** The conv's byte floor at a micro-batch of 4 x 256 tokens is about 33.6 MB a pass (1,024 x 8,192 bf16 in and out), 0.16 ms at 209.5 GB/s, about 0.64 ms per layer over forward, recompute and the two gradients, about 15 ms for the 24 layers of a micro-batch; a generic library kernel at 1 to 30 percent of bandwidth would make it 2 to 50 ms per layer, 0.05 to 1.2 s of a 12.5 s micro-batch. Source: arithmetic on [S121]'s shapes and [L10] `copy.d2d.size_1gib`.
  - Implication: the conv is the one fallback whose floor is tiny and whose real cost is unknown; if WF2 finds it near the top of the range it is a cheap fix (fla's Triton conv or a `causal-conv1d` build, TS10) that does not wait on the Gated DeltaNet binding.
  - Candidate experiment: WF2, lever `impl` = `torch` (`F.conv1d` with groups = 8,192, padding 3, sliced to causal, which is what `nn.Conv1d` dispatches to MIOpen) against `fla` (`fla.modules.conv` Triton kernel, skipped when fla is absent), forward plus backward at 256 and 1,024 tokens, the first call per shape recorded apart. Expected movement: torch 2 to 50 ms per layer forward plus backward, at least 3x the harness's byte floor for its three passes (forward, input gradient, weight gradient: 0.48 ms at 256 tokens); fla within 3x of that floor. Minimum sample size: 5 repeats per case after 2 warm-up calls. Confidence: low (0.4). Kill criterion: torch within 3x of that floor at both lengths.

## 5. Kernel class 3: the LoRA adapter GEMMs

- **[opinion]** The adapter A forward, `[1024, 2560] x [2560, 16]`, has an output 16 columns wide, one 16-wide WMMA tile; a library tile of 64 rows gives 16 workgroups and one of 128 rows gives 8, under the 20 WGPs the chip has, so most of the chip idles; its input is 5.24 MB, a byte floor of 25 us at 209.5 GB/s, against the 53 us the chip sheet measured. The B forward (19 us) already beats its DRAM floor for writing 5.24 MB (25 us), so it is served from the 32 MiB last-level cache and has no lever. Splitting A's K = 2560 four ways gives 64 workgroups and a reduction of four 1,024 x 16 float32 partials (256 KiB), predicted 53 to 25 to 30 us. Source: arithmetic on [L10] (`lora.bf16.lora4b.k2560_n2560.a_fwd` 0.053 ms, `b_fwd` 0.019 ms, `cache.rocminfo.l3_bytes` 33,554,432, `cache.hip.compute_units` 20) and the WMMA shape in `strix_halo_hardware.md` section 6 ([S80], [S82]).
  - Implication: on today's step split-K saves at most about 28 us x 2 (forward and recompute) x 248 adapters, 14 ms of a 12.5 s micro-batch (0.1 percent); it is ranked last now and becomes worth a run only when the step is near 1 s per micro-batch, where the adapters' 70 ms are about 7 percent. Fusing A and B into one kernel removes 248 x 2 launches per pass, which WF3's per-op cost prices.
  - Candidate experiment: WF5, the harness's `lora` class: a Triton A-projection kernel, lever `split_k` 1 against 4 (`BLOCK_M` 64, `num_warps` 4), beside `torch.nn.functional.linear` as the reference row, at the chip sheet's shape and at the MLP's 9,216-wide input. Expected movement: A forward 53 to 25 to 30 us at split-K 4; torch's row reproduces the chip sheet's 0.053 ms within 20 percent. Minimum sample size: 5 repeats per case. Confidence: medium (0.5). Kill criterion: split-K 4 under 1.3x faster than split-K 1.

## 6. Kernel class 4: dense GEMMs, attention and the elementwise tail

- **[measured]** The dense bf16 GEMMs run at 42.3 to 42.7 TFLOP/s at the step's shapes through the library's own tiles (a 1,024 x 2,560 output in 128 x 128 tiles is 160 workgroups, 8 per WGP). Source: [L10] `lora.bf16.lora4b.k2560_n2560.fwd` and `dgrad`.
  - Implication: none of R42's wavefront levers apply (the tile, wave size and occupancy belong to hipBLASLt or Tensile); their lever is TunableOp (R36's TS lever, `lab/train.py --tunableop`), and their share of today's step is predicted at 4 to 20 percent (section 1).
  - Candidate experiment: none beyond R34's achieved-matmul-rate measurement. Expected movement: GEMMs at 30 to 42 TFLOP/s during the step. Minimum sample size: 5 profiled steps. Confidence: medium (0.55).

- **[practitioner]** The trainer pins `attn_implementation` to SDPA and keeps AOTriton's experimental gfx1151 kernels off unless `--aotriton-experimental` is passed; the student's 8 full-attention layers have 16 query heads of dimension 256. Source: `lab/train.py::PINNED` and the `--aotriton-experimental` flag (main at 69aa5b4); [S121] `configuration_qwen3_5.py` (`head_dim` 256).
  - Implication: at 256 tokens the attention scores of a layer are 4 x 16 x 256 x 256 float32 = 16.8 MB, under 0.1 ms of traffic, so attention is predicted under 1 percent of the step whichever kernel runs it; no R42 run is spent on it until R34 says otherwise.
  - Candidate experiment: none beyond R36's TS6 (the AOTriton flag). Expected movement: under 1 percent of a step. Minimum sample size: 3 runs per arm of 50 steps. Confidence: medium (0.6).

- **[opinion]** The elementwise tail (RMS norms, rotary, gates, SiLU, casts, the float32 loss over 248,320 logits) is a launch-count problem, not a wavefront one: each op is a separate memory-bound kernel whose floor is microseconds, so its cost per micro-batch is about kernel count x per-op cost; fla v0.5.2 ships fused versions (`fla/modules/layernorm.py`, `fused_norm_gate.py`, `fused_linear_cross_entropy.py`). Source: [S183] those module files; the per-op cost is WF3's (section 2); HIP graphs doc [S185] for why framework launch cost can exceed kernel time.
  - Implication: launch-count reduction for the tail (fused norms and loss, HIP graph capture, torch.compile after fla) is priced by WF3 times R34's kernel count before any of it is built.
  - Experiment: WF3, as specified in section 2.
