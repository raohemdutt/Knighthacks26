# One LoRA training step of the student on gfx1151: the three predicted time sinks, their arithmetic floors, and what each change should buy in joules per sample

Prediction note for the kernels track, written for Task Board task R37 (thinking sitting 1) on 2026-10-11. It covers one optimizer step of the L3 trainer (`lab/train.py`) on the student, Qwen3.5-4B in bf16 with PEFT LoRA r 16 on every language-model linear, on the trainer pod (Strix Halo, Radeon 8060S, gfx1151, ROCm 7.13, torch 2.9.1+rocm7.13.0, Triton 3.5.1, pod D, node pod-b). It states the three places a step is expected to spend its time, the arithmetic floor of each from the R33 chip sheet, the measurement the R34 step profile must take to confirm each, and a predicted gain, confidence and kill criterion for the change aimed at each. Inference, the vision tower (excluded from LoRA by the trainer) and the choice of training recipe are out of scope.

**This is a prediction ahead of the profile.** R34, the training-step profile, has not run, and no training step of the student has been measured on gfx1151: the one committed training measurement of this model is the smoke run on an R9700 (gfx1201), another chip (`lab/refine/metrics.jsonl:13`). So even the "now" row below is a prediction, and the R34 profile (or the first L3 run, which records `trainer.samples_per_s` and `trainer.energy_j`) replaces it before any "after" row is judged.

Watts are taken at 110 W, the middle of the 102 to 127 W the chip sheet's LoRA GEMMs drew on pod D; at 125 W every J/sample is 1.14 times higher. Each change is one lever against the pinned baseline (kernels rubric K2); the last row is the three together, which is not a measured or independent product.

## 1. The step being predicted

- **[practitioner]** The L3 trainer's defaults are R36's pinned baseline: LoRA r 16, alpha 32, dropout 0 on all-linear with the vision modules excluded; learning rate 2e-4; per-device batch 4 by gradient accumulation 4 (effective batch 16); gradient checkpointing on with `use_reentrant` False; SDPA attention; `adamw_torch_fused`; two forkserver DataLoader workers; Gated DeltaNet and causal conv1d on their torch paths (`BASELINE["gdn_kernel"] = "torch"`); no packing, no length grouping, no torch.compile; and it records `trainer.samples_per_s`, `trainer.mean_socket_w` and `trainer.energy_j` per run. Source: `lab/train.py` module docstring ("Settings", "Per step") and `BASELINE`, `PINNED`, `VISION_MODULES` (main at e81bccd).
  - Implication: joules per sample is directly readable from one L3 run (`trainer.energy_j` over samples seen), so every row of the Summary table has a measuring stick already in the trainer; no new harness is needed.
  - Candidate experiment: TP0, the pinned-baseline run (TS1 of the settings note) on pod D for 50 optimizer steps after 5 warm-up steps at a fixed 256-token length, with the torch profiler on for 5 of the steps (R34). Expected movement: none against itself; it replaces the predicted "now" of 0.40 samples/s and 275 J/sample. Minimum sample size: 3 runs of 50 optimizer steps. Confidence: medium (0.6) that it fits one 60-minute training slot.

- **[practitioner]** The student's text model has 32 layers in the pattern three Gated DeltaNet layers then one full-attention layer (24 and 8), hidden size 2560, MLP width 9216, 16 query heads and 4 KV heads of dimension 256 with an output gate, linear-attention key heads 16 x 128 and value heads 32 x 128, a 248,320-entry vocabulary with tied embeddings, and `mamba_ssm_dtype` float32. In transformers the Gated DeltaNet layer has `in_proj_qkv` (2560 to 2 x 2048 + 4096), `in_proj_z` (2560 to 4096), `in_proj_b` and `in_proj_a` (2560 to 32 each) and `out_proj` (4096 to 2560), and the attention layer's `q_proj` is 2560 to 16 x 256 x 2. Source: [S181] `Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B` `config.json` (opened 2026-10-11); [S121] transformers `modeling_qwen3_5.py` at 536ecc0, `Qwen3_5GatedDeltaNet.__init__` and the attention layer's projections (re-opened 2026-10-11).
  - Implication: the language-model linears hold about 3.57 billion weights (24 x 112.9 M + 8 x 107.5 M, derived: MLP 70.8 M, Gated DeltaNet projections 42.1 M, attention projections 36.7 M per layer) and the tied embedding 0.64 billion; PEFT's all-linear wraps 248 of them (8 per Gated DeltaNet layer, 7 per attention layer, derived), which sets the count of LoRA adapter kernels in finding 4.
  - Candidate experiment: none beyond TP0, which prints the trainable parameter count. Expected movement: none in J/sample; trainable parameters about 32.5 M at r 16 (derived from the 248 adapter shapes). Minimum sample size: 1 run. Confidence: high (0.8).

## 2. Sink 1: the Gated DeltaNet torch reference

- **[practitioner]** With no flash-linear-attention in the image, the 24 Gated DeltaNet layers run `torch_chunk_gated_delta_rule`: it casts query, key, value, beta and decay to float32, pads the sequence to a multiple of 64, builds per-chunk 64 x 64 pairwise-decay and intra-chunk matrices, solves two unit lower-triangular systems per chunk with `torch.linalg.solve_triangular`, then walks the chunks one at a time in a Python loop (three matmuls and a state update per chunk), and casts the output back. Source: [S121] transformers `modeling_qwen3_5.py` at 536ecc0, `torch_chunk_gated_delta_rule` (re-opened 2026-10-11); `lab/refine/research/kernels/settings_training_gfx1151.md` section 6 (the fallback is bound because the image has no `fla`).
  - Implication: under autograd every float32 intermediate is kept for backward, gradient checkpointing runs the whole function twice, and the backward of each triangular solve is another solve; the work is many small float32 kernels per layer rather than one fused kernel, which is the shape of work this chip runs worst (finding 4).
  - Experiment: T1, as specified in finding 6.

- **[opinion]** At a micro-batch of 4 sequences of 256 tokens each float32 activation of a Gated DeltaNet layer is 4 x 32 x 256 x 128 x 4 bytes = 16.8 MB; if one forward pass reads or writes 20 to 30 such tensors, the 24 layers move 32 to 48 GB per micro-batch over forward, recompute and a backward counted as twice a forward, which at 209.5 GB/s is 38 to 58 ms per sample; the layer's matrix work is small next to the projections. The arithmetic floor of sink 1 is therefore well under the projections' 145 ms per sample (finding 3), so if R34 finds the Gated DeltaNet layers at 30 to 60 percent of a step, as predicted here, the cost is launch count, host time and the batched small solves, not bytes. Predicted share of a step: 45 percent (30 to 60). Source: arithmetic on [S121]'s tensor shapes, [S181]'s head counts and `lab/refine/research/kernels/data/chipsheet-gfx1151-2026-10-11.json` (`copy.d2d.size_1gib`, 209.5 GB/s); the learning note's prediction LR-1 that these layers take more than half of a step (`lab/refine/research/learning/lora_recipe.md:34`).
  - Implication: a fused kernel removes most of this sink whatever its exact cause, which is why T1's predicted gain depends mostly on the share R34 measures: 30 percent gives 1.35x samples per second, 60 percent gives 2.06x (finding 6).
  - Candidate experiment: R34 measurement M1: the torch profiler over 5 optimizer steps with `record_function` ranges per decoder layer type (Gated DeltaNet, attention, MLP) in forward, recompute and backward, plus rocprofv3's kernel trace for kernel counts; `lab/trace_profile.py` reads both as it is (`lab/README.md`, "Decode profile" section, last paragraph). Expected movement: none in J/sample; Gated DeltaNet layers 30 to 60 percent of the step, at least 1,000 kernels per micro-batch in them, `solve_triangular` kernels among the top 10. Minimum sample size: 5 profiled steps after 5 warm-up steps. Confidence: medium (0.5).

## 3. Sink 2: the base GEMMs, with the checkpointing recompute and the lm_head

- **[measured]** On pod D the bf16 GEMMs of a 4B LoRA step at 1,024 tokens (4 x 256) and K = N = 2560 ran at 42.3 TFLOP/s forward and 42.7 TFLOP/s for the input gradient (0.318 and 0.314 ms), drawing 122.4 and 126.5 W on average; the LoRA adapter GEMMs of the same projection ran at 1.58 (A forward, `[1024,2560] x [2560,16]`, 0.053 ms), 4.50 (B forward, 0.019 ms), 2.47 (A weight gradient, 0.034 ms) and 2.03 TFLOP/s (B weight gradient, 0.041 ms), at 102 to 110 W. Source: `lab/refine/research/kernels/data/chipsheet-gfx1151-2026-10-11.json`, section "lora" (`lora.bf16.lora4b.k2560_n2560.*`, 15 repeats each, through `torch.nn.functional.linear`).
  - Implication: the dense GEMMs run near the best rate measured on this chip, and the adapters run 10 to 27 times slower per FLOP because their rank-16 side is too thin to fill a tile; the watts under GEMM load (102 to 127 W) are the basis of the 110 W in the Summary.
  - Experiment: none beyond R34 measurement M2 (next finding).

- **[opinion]** Per trained token the frozen base does a forward (2 x 3.57 G FLOP), a recompute of that forward under checkpointing (2 x 3.57 G) and an input-gradient pass (2 x 3.57 G), and the tied lm_head a forward and an input gradient (4 x 0.64 G): about 24.0 GFLOP per token, 6.13 TFLOP per 256-token sample, 145 ms per sample at 42.3 TFLOP/s, a floor of about 6.9 samples per second and 16 J per sample at 110 W. Predicted share of a step: 10 percent (6 to 20), because the only committed measurement of this step (0.51 samples/s on an R9700, a chip with about three times this one's copy rate) is about 13 times slower than this floor. Source: arithmetic on finding 1's shapes ([S181], [S121]) and the chip sheet rate of the previous finding; `lab/refine/metrics.jsonl:13` (R9700 smoke run, `lab/smoke_lora.py`, batch 4, 256 tokens, 20 steps, another chip, never a gfx1151 baseline).
  - Implication: GEMM tuning levers (TunableOp, hipBLASLt) can move at most this share, which is why they rank below the three changes here (they are ranks 8 and 10 of the settings note's shortlist); checkpointing off removes the recompute, a quarter of these FLOPs, and the same fraction of every other sink (T2, finding 7).
  - Candidate experiment: R34 measurement M2: total time in the GEMM kernels (Tensile `Cijk_*` or hipBLASLt names) split by the forward, recompute and backward ranges of M1, with achieved TFLOP/s against 42.3. Expected movement: none in J/sample; GEMMs 6 to 20 percent of a step at 30 to 42 TFLOP/s. Minimum sample size: 5 profiled steps. Confidence: medium (0.55).

## 4. Sink 3: the small-kernel tail, LoRA adapters included

- **[opinion]** Each of the 248 wrapped linears adds at least four adapter GEMMs per micro-batch (A and B forward, recomputed under checkpointing, A and B weight gradients, and two input gradients through the adapter), about 0.29 ms per linear at the chip sheet's times, 72 ms per micro-batch or 18 ms per sample; around them sit the RMS norms, rotary embedding, gates, SiLU, casts and the loss over a 248,320-entry vocabulary (a float32 softmax over 1,024 x 248,320 logits is about 1 GB per pass), each a separate kernel in eager PyTorch. The bytes and FLOPs of this tail are small; its cost is per-kernel launch and host time, roughly the same per micro-batch whatever the micro-batch size. Predicted share of a step: 35 percent (20 to 45). Source: arithmetic on `lab/refine/research/kernels/data/chipsheet-gfx1151-2026-10-11.json` (`lora.bf16.lora4b.k2560_n2560.a_fwd`, `b_fwd`, `a_wgrad`, `b_wgrad`), finding 1's 248 adapters and [S181]'s vocabulary; `lab/train.py` (`torch_compile=False`, no fused LoRA kernel in the settings it passes).
  - Implication: this sink falls per sample when more samples share each kernel launch (T3, a larger micro-batch at the same effective batch) and when kernels are fused (torch.compile, rank 7 of the settings note, after T1); it is the reason the predicted "now" sits so far above the GEMM floor besides sink 1.
  - Candidate experiment: R34 measurement M3: kernels per micro-batch, the median kernel duration, and the GPU-idle time between kernels inside a step (host-bound gaps) from the rocprofv3 trace, with the adapter kernels (`[1024,16]` and `[16,2560]` shapes) summed separately. Expected movement: none in J/sample; over 10,000 kernels per micro-batch, median under 50 us, idle gaps 15 to 40 percent of a step. Minimum sample size: 5 profiled steps. Confidence: low (0.4).

## 5. The predicted baseline

- **[opinion]** Adding the three sinks (45, 10 and 35 percent, with 10 percent for the optimizer, data loading and everything else) to the only measured step of this model gives the predicted gfx1151 baseline of 0.40 samples/s (0.25 to 0.60), 2.5 s per sample, about 275 J per sample at 110 W (183 to 440); the R9700 smoke step ran at 0.51 samples/s, and this chip has about a third of its copy rate and fewer CUs, but most of the step is predicted to be latency-bound small work where the gap between the chips is smaller than the bandwidth ratio. Source: `lab/refine/metrics.jsonl:13` and `:14` (R9700 smoke, 0.51 samples/s, 8.7 GiB peak); `lab/refine/research/kernels/data/chipsheet-gfx1151-2026-10-11.json` (`copy.d2d.size_1gib`, the `lora.*` watts).
  - Implication: this row is the weakest number in the note (confidence 0.35): it fixes the units and the order of the Summary table, not a value anyone should quote; TP0 replaces it in the first morning slot, and every "after" row scales with it because each is predicted as a ratio.
  - Candidate experiment: TP0, as specified in finding 1. Expected movement: `trainer.samples_per_s` 0.25 to 0.60 and J/sample 183 to 440 on the pinned baseline. Minimum sample size: 3 runs of 50 optimizer steps. Confidence: low (0.35).

## 6. T1: Gated DeltaNet on flash-linear-attention

- **[opinion]** Binding `fla`'s `chunk_gated_delta_rule` (installed into the user site with `--no-deps`, torch and Triton unchanged) replaces sink 1's many float32 kernels with fused Triton kernels in forward and backward; if those run 5 to 10 times faster than the reference (central 7), the step shrinks to (1 - share) + share / 7 of today: 1.35x at a 30 percent share, 1.63x at 45 percent and 2.06x at 60 percent, so 0.40 to about 0.65 samples/s (0.54 to 0.82) and 275 to about 169 J/sample (134 to 204) at 110 W. Source: arithmetic on finding 2's share; `lab/refine/research/kernels/settings_training_gfx1151.md` section 6 (TS7: the install that keeps the stack pinned, fla's ROCm claim, the cold-cache first-run latency on an RDNA 3.5 part) and section 14 (rank 1, up 1.5x to 3x).
  - Implication: the largest single cut in joules per sample on the list, and the gate for packing (TS9) and torch.compile (TS16); its risk is the install and the first-run Triton compile on gfx1151, not the arithmetic.
  - Candidate experiment: T1 (TS7), arms pinned baseline against fla bound, at a fixed 256-token length, with the binding confirmed from the bound function and the fallback warning absent, and one fixed batch's LoRA gradients compared between the arms before any timing. Expected movement: `trainer.samples_per_s` 0.40 to 0.54 to 0.82 (central 0.65), joules per sample 275 to 134 to 204 (central 169), peak memory down 10 to 30 percent. Minimum sample size: 3 runs per arm of 50 optimizer steps after 10 warm-up steps. Confidence: medium (0.5). Kill criterion: stop before timing if the install would change `torch.__version__` or `triton.__version__`, or if the fla gradients on the fixed batch differ from the reference beyond fla's own test tolerance; drop after timing if R34 shows the Gated DeltaNet layers under 20 percent of a step, if `verdict()` does not read the fla arm ahead by more than 15 percent in samples per second, or if the 50-step loss leaves the baseline's band.

## 7. T2: gradient checkpointing off

- **[opinion]** Checkpointing runs every layer's forward twice, so with forward, recompute and a backward of about twice a forward the step is about four forward-equivalents; turning it off removes one, an upper bound of 1.33x, and transformers' own figure for checkpointing is about 20 percent slower training. Predicted: 1.2x (1.1 to 1.3), 0.40 to about 0.48 samples/s, 275 to about 229 J/sample (212 to 250); peak memory up from under 10 GB toward 15 to 25 GB at micro-batch 4, inside the pod's shared 64 GB. Source: arithmetic on findings 2 to 4; `lab/refine/research/kernels/settings_training_gfx1151.md` section 9 (TRL's default of checkpointing on, transformers' "~20% slower training", TS12) and section 13; `lab/train.py` (`--no-gradient-checkpointing`).
  - Implication: a one-flag change with the highest confidence on the list and no numerics change (the same forward, kept instead of recomputed); smaller than T1, so it is the fallback for the first slot, and it stacks with T1 and T3.
  - Candidate experiment: T2 (TS12), arms pinned baseline against `--no-gradient-checkpointing`. Expected movement: `trainer.samples_per_s` up 10 to 30 percent (central 20), joules per sample down 9 to 23 percent (central 17), peak memory up 1.5x to 3x. Minimum sample size: 3 runs per arm of 50 optimizer steps. Confidence: medium (0.6). Kill criterion: drop if `torch.cuda.mem_get_info()` leaves under 10 percent free at the peak, or if `verdict()` does not read the arm ahead by more than 8 percent in samples per second.

## 8. T3: a larger micro-batch at the same effective batch

- **[opinion]** At the same effective batch of 16, micro-batch 16 with accumulation 1 launches each kernel of the step once per optimizer step instead of four times; if the tail of finding 4 is 35 percent of a step and mostly per-launch cost, it falls to a quarter per sample, 1.36x (1.18x at a 20 percent tail, 1.5x at 45 percent), while the GEMMs and the Gated DeltaNet work per sample stay roughly the same. Predicted: 0.40 to about 0.54 samples/s (0.47 to 0.60), 275 to about 203 J/sample (182 to 234). Source: arithmetic on finding 4; `lab/refine/research/kernels/settings_training_gfx1151.md` section 9 (TS13, the effective batch held fixed, transformers' guidance against many accumulation steps); `lab/train.py` (`--batch` with `--grad-accum`).
  - Implication: it attacks the tail without new code, and it is what makes the tail visible: if T3 gains under 10 percent, the tail is not launch-bound and torch.compile (the other tail lever) drops in rank.
  - Candidate experiment: T3 (TS13), arms batch 4 x accumulation 4 against 16 x 1 (8 x 2 as a middle arm if 16 does not fit), checkpointing on in both. Expected movement: `trainer.samples_per_s` up 15 to 50 percent (central 36), joules per sample down 13 to 34 percent (central 26), activation memory up about 4x. Minimum sample size: 3 runs per arm of 50 optimizer steps. Confidence: low to medium (0.45). Kill criterion: drop if micro-batch 16 runs out of memory and 8 gains under 10 percent, or if `verdict()` does not read it ahead by more than 10 percent, or if the 50-step loss leaves the baseline's band.

## Open questions

- Whether `torch.linalg.solve_triangular` on float32 batches of 64 x 64 runs as one batched kernel or many on ROCm 7.13; M1's kernel counts answer it, and it decides how much of sink 1 is the solves.
- Which fla release has a Triton floor at or below 3.5.1, as the settings note asks; T1 cannot start without it.
- The real length distribution of L3's rows: every number here assumes 256 tokens per sample (the smoke script's and the chip sheet's), and the trainer pads per micro-batch, so length grouping (TS14) may matter more than T3 on real data.
- Whether socket power under training stays near the chip sheet's 102 to 127 W or sits at the 96 W plateau R26 saw under decode.
