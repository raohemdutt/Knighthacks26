# Can a newer vLLM close the gfx1151 teacher's decode gap, and what is the smallest upgrade that keeps our patch?

This note audits vLLM's history after the commit the teacher runs (b1388b1, which is the v0.19.1 tag) for the changes that bear on batch-1 decode on the Strix Halo (gfx1151, RDNA 3.5): the ROCm platform code, the Triton unified attention kernel, the W4A16 (AWQ, GPTQ, compressed-tensors, Quark INT4) decode kernels, and the RDNA 3.5 and RDNA 4 (gfx12) targets. It records where each of the nine hunks of `lab/vllm-gfx1201.diff` stops applying, names the smallest upgrade, estimates the rebuild cost on a pod, and predicts the decode gain against the 2.88 tok/s we measure and the 7.5 tok/s the public prior gives vLLM on this chip. It does not measure anything: no pod was touched, and R58's llama.cpp Vulkan number on the pod is the other half of the comparison.

## Summary

- The build is older than the lab thinks. b1388b1 is exactly the `v0.19.1` tag on a release branch (2026-04-17); the "0.19.2" in our version string is the `SETUPTOOLS_SCM_PRETEND_VERSION` the build script sets. There is no `v0.19.3`; the next tags are `v0.19.2rc0` (never released) and `v0.20.0`.
- Every tag after v0.19.1 breaks the patch, but almost all of it is obsolete. Of the nine hunks, three are build pins that `tools/use_existing_torch.py` now strips, one (`compat.cuh`) was fixed upstream in v0.21.0, one (`rocm.py` warning) in v0.27.0, and one (`__init__.py` WSL fallback) was adopted upstream in v0.29.0 but gated on WSL, so it needs a one-line variant on the pod. The two `num_stages=1` hunks have no upstream equivalent; main exposes `launch_num_stages`, so they become one line.
- The decode fix exists upstream and is not in any build we have. `RDNAHybridW4A16LinearKernel` (#40977, in v0.26.0) replaces the Conch Triton fallback with a HIP skinny GEMM at M <= 5 and was measured by its author on gfx1151 at 3.4x to 4.8x lower TPOT across four W4A16 models. Its follow-ups (#54965 in v0.30.0; #56301 and #52619 unreleased, 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-07) and the opt-in `ROCM_SEGMENTED_ATTN` backend (#59132, unreleased, 2026-10-06; 5.49 to 10.79 tok/s single-stream on a 27B W4A16 model on gfx1151) complete the path.
- Smallest upgrade: `v0.26.0` is the first release with the kernel; `v0.31.0` (2026-10-02) is the best tagged release; the recommended pin is main at or after `5281e4990` (2026-10-07), which carries all of the above. Rebuild cost: about 5 to 10 minutes of compile on a pod's local disk plus a cold torch.compile warm-up of about 9 minutes, with one real risk (the image's torch 2.9.1 against a tree that expects 2.13).
- Prediction: 7 to 10 tok/s at batch 1 on the short prompt after the upgrade (confidence 0.6), and about 11 tok/s at deep context with the segmented attention backend on, against the 7.5 tok/s prior and the 12 to 14 tok/s llama.cpp band. Upgrade first; switch engines only if the upgraded build fails on the image's torch or lands under about 7 tok/s.

## Where the nine hunks stop applying

- **[measured]** At `v0.19.1` all nine hunks apply (`git apply --cached --check` clean). At `v0.19.2rc0`, `v0.20.0` and `v0.20.2`, six hunks fail: H1 and H2 because `pyproject.toml` now pins `torch == 2.11.0` and the `[tool.uv]` context moved; H3 and H4 because `requirements/build.txt` and `requirements/rocm-build.txt` no longer exist (they became the `requirements/build/` directory, `requirements/build/rocm.txt` pinning `torch==2.11.0` from the rocm7.1 index); H7 and H8 because `2ce3d0ce3` "[Feature] KV cache per-token-head INT8/FP8 quantization" (#38378) and then #40631 changed the launch arguments. H5, H6 (offset 38 to 39 lines) and H9 apply. Source: [S172] `git apply --cached --reject` per tag, `git ls-tree <tag> requirements/`
