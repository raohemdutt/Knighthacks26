Track: telemetry. Opened and written 2026-10-10 by telemetry refinement pass 1. Scope: how a request window stamped on one node is matched with power samples stamped on another, and what an integral does at the window's edges when samples are sparse or deduplicated. ## 1. Matching pod samples to Jetson request times

- **[practitioner]** WC-01. NTP version 4 computes the offset of a server B relative to a client A from four timestamps, T1 (request sent, on A's clock), T2 (request received, on B's clock), T3 (reply sent, on B's clock) and T4 (reply received, on A's clock), as theta = 1/2 x [(T2 - T1) + (T3 - T4)], and the round-trip delay as delta = (T4 - T1) - (T3 - T2) (Section 8); it defines the synchronization distance, epsilon + delta / 2, as the maximum error due to all causes, epsilon being the maximum error inherent in the measurement (Section 4). Citation: D. Mills, J. Martin (Ed.), J. Burbank and W. Kasch, "Network Time Protocol Version 4: Protocol and Algorithms Specification", RFC 5905, IETF Standards Track, June 2010, Sections 4 and 8, https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5905 [S44]

  Implies for our metrics: pod power samples are stamped on the pod while request windows are stamped on the Jetson (Context: Telemetry reads the pod samplers through the hub proxy), so joules per answer on a pod needs the pod-minus-Jetson offset; one four-timestamp exchange through the proxy gives it, with an error of at most about half the round trip, and that bound belongs beside every pod number (rubric TT2, one clock). At the teacher's 200 W, 100 ms of misalignment at one edge moves about 20 J, about 2% of a 1.1 kJ caption (`attribution-and-baselines.md` AT-20).

  Candidate experiment: pod A through the hub proxy, requests only: 50 four-timestamp exchanges against the pod sampler's endpoint spread over 10 minutes, recording theta and delta for each. Expected movement: delta (the proxy round trip) in the tens to a few hundred milliseconds, and theta steady within delta / 2 across the 10 minutes; the median delta / 2 becomes the stated clock error of every pod window edge. Minimum sample size: 50 exchanges.

## 2. What an integral does at the window's edges

- **[practitioner]** WC-02. Zeus's `PowerMonitor.get_energy(start_time, end_time)` integrates the power samples whose timestamps fall inside the window (`get_power_timeline` skips samples before the start and after the end, and nothing is interpolated at the edges), returns 0.0 for a GPU with fewer than two samples in the window, and the monitor's polling loop sends a GPU reading only when the power changed (code comment "Deduplication: only send if power changed"). Citation: Zeus API reference, `zeus.monitor.power`, `PowerMonitor.get_energy` and `get_power_timeline`, https://ml.energy/zeus/reference/monitor/power/ [S45]

  Implies for our metrics: an integral of in-window samples only drops the partial interval at each edge, up to one sampling period per edge, which is most of the window for a 2 to 5 s answer sampled once a second, and a deduplicated trace can leave a window inside a steady stretch with no sample at all, so it reads 0 J. Joules per answer therefore carries the last sample at or before `started` and the first at or after `ended` into the integral, clipped to the window by linear interpolation, and never deduplicates readings before it attributes them (rubric TT2, the edge rule; T4).

  Candidate experiment: fixture on this machine, no node: replay a synthetic 1 Hz power trace, deduplicated and not, against 50 request windows of 1 to 6 s, computing joules per answer with the in-window-only rule and with the bracketing rule, against the same load sampled every 10 ms. Expected movement: the in-window-only rule falls short by up to two sampling periods of energy per window, most on the shortest windows, and reads 0 J on some deduplicated windows; the bracketing rule stays within 10% of the 10 ms reference. Minimum sample size: 50 windows.
