# Power samplers on our nodes: what the R9700 and the Orin Nano report, how fast, and with what error

Track: telemetry. Opened and written 2026-10-10. Scope: how our two kinds of node report power (AMD SMI and the amdgpu driver on the R9700 pods; tegrastats and the INA3221 sensor on the Jetson Orin Nano), how fast and how faithfully those readings follow the load, and what the samplers themselves cost. Companion notes: `attribution-and-baselines.md` (turning samples into joules per answer) and `power-and-clock-levers.md` (caps, clocks and power modes). Every source is listed in `SOURCES.md` with its URL or DOI and the date it was opened; bracketed IDs such as [S01] point there.

How to read a finding. Each finding is one bullet. It opens with its label and an ID (PS-01 and so on), states what was found with its numbers and setup, and ends with its citation. Under it, "Implies for our metrics" says what it means for our metric set, and "Candidate experiment" names the node, the procedure, the expected movement and the minimum sample size. A pass cites a finding by file and ID, for example `power-sampling.md PS-03`.

Labels. `measured`: a number someone measured, with the setup stated. `practitioner`: a documented statement or recommendation from the people who build or run the tool (vendor documentation, driver source, maintainer replies, benchmark methodology). `anecdote`: a single user's report. `opinion`: argument without measurement.

Our metric set (the telemetry rubric and the Telemetry context spec): joules per answer per node, idle watts per node, sampler overhead (CPU percent and GPU interference), samples per attributed window, and attribution error against a reference reading; in the metrics file these appear as `teacher.watts_avg`, `teacher.watts_peak`, `teacher.joules_per_token`, `pod.idle_watts` and so on. The samplers publish `telemetry.gpu.power` (`socket_w`, `gfx_mhz`, `mem_mhz`, `throttled`), `telemetry.jetson.power` (`rails` in mW, `nvpmodel`) and `telemetry.request` (`started`, `ended`, `tokens_in`, `tokens_out`).

Minimum sample sizes. Unpaired comparisons use n per arm = 15.7 x (CV / d)^2, rounded up and never below 5, where CV is the run-to-run coefficient of variation of the metric, d is the relative change to detect, and 15.7 = 2 x (1.96 + 0.84)^2 (two-sided alpha 0.05, power 0.8). Paired comparisons (the same prompts in both arms) use 7.85 x (CV / d)^2 pairs. Sensor characterizations (lag, update period) use 10 repeated steps, which puts the 95% confidence half-width at about 0.7 standard deviations. Until we have measured our own CVs, per-answer energy is assumed to have CV = 10% and steady-state window energy CV = 5%. This is an assumption, not a finding: 5% is the acceptance band Vellaisamy et al. use between repeated windows [S32] and close to the 4.89% residual error Yang et al. report after good practice [S28]. The first run of any experiment below replaces the assumption with our measured CV.

## 1. The R9700 through AMD SMI and the amdgpu driver

### Takeaway

AMD documents only an averaged socket power for Radeon (Navi) cards, and the instantaneous field only for MI300 and later (PS-01). The upstream amdgpu driver for RDNA4's power firmware exposes exactly one power number, the firmware's average socket power in whole watts (PS-02), and reports the hardware energy counter only on SMU 14.0.3 with power firmware 0x00685000 or newer, through a change committed in September 2026 (PS-03). Our pods report gfx1201, the RX 9070 family, which the kernel lists as SMU 14.0.3, so whether the counter is live on them depends on the pod's driver and firmware, and that is a five-minute check. Where AMD sensors have been measured against references, the averaged field lagged a load step by up to seconds while energy-counter differences followed it within milliseconds (PS-06), and an RDNA3 card's built-in energy matched an external meter closely (PS-07). No published study measures the R9700's sensor, so our own step test is the first evidence we would have; until it runs, any pod joules-per-answer number integrated from the averaged field over a window of a few seconds is low confidence.

### Cited findings

- **[practitioner]** PS-01. AMD SMI documents `average_socket_power` as "Average socket power in W, Navi + Mi 200 and earlier Series cards" and `current_socket_power` as "Current socket power in W, Mi 300+ Series cards", and states that "Unsupported struct members are set to UINT32_MAX". Citation: AMD, AMD SMI library reference, `amdsmi_power_info_t` struct, ROCm documentation 7.2.3, https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/amdsmi/en/docs-7.2.3/doxygen/docBin/html/structamdsmi__power__info__t.html [S01]

  Implies for our metrics: the `SOCKET_POWER` our pod sampler reads on the R9700 is an averaged value, so `telemetry.gpu.power` should record which field it came from (average or current), joules per answer integrated from it inherits the averaging lag as attribution error, and a UINT32_MAX or "N/A" reading must be stored as missing, never as a number.

  Candidate experiment: pod B, one queued 15-minute slot, one script together with PS-03 and PS-05. Poll `amdsmi_get_power_info` every 10 ms with `AMDSMI_GPU_METRICS_CACHE_MS=0` through 10 square waves of 5 s full load (a GEMM loop) and 5 s idle. Expected movement: `current_socket_power` is unsupported, and `average_socket_power` needs 0.5 to 3 s to rise from 10% to 90% of its plateau (the band between the MI300A and MI250X results in PS-06). Minimum sample size: 10 steps.

- **[practitioner]** PS-02. In the upstream amdgpu driver for SMU 14.0.2 and 14.0.3 (RDNA4), the only power sensor is `AMDGPU_PP_SENSOR_GPU_AVG_POWER`, read from the firmware's `AverageSocketPower` and multiplied by `MILLIWATT_PER_WATT` (so the source value is whole watts); the file defines no input (instantaneous) power sensor, and `gpu_metrics->average_socket_power` is filled from the same field. The kernel documents `power1_average` as "average power used by the SoC in microWatts" and `power1_input` as "instantaneous power used by the SoC in microWatts", and `gpu_metrics` as "a snapshot of all sensors at the same time". Citation: Linux kernel source, drivers/gpu/drm/amd/pm/swsmu/smu14/smu_v14_0_2_ppt.c (torvalds/linux master, read 2026-10-10), https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/gpu/drm/amd/pm/swsmu/smu14/smu_v14_0_2_ppt.c [S05]; Linux kernel documentation, "drm/amdgpu thermal and power", hwmon interfaces and GPU metrics sections, https://docs.kernel.org/gpu/amdgpu/thermal.html [S04]

  Implies for our metrics: on the pods `socket_w` has 1 W resolution, which is 2.5% of the ~40 W idle and 0.5% of the ~200 W teacher average (PS-11, PS-12), so idle watts per node should be reported to the whole watt; hwmon `power1_input` will be missing, so a sampler written to expect it reads nothing; one `gpu_metrics` read per sample gives power, clocks and throttle status from the same instant, which is what one `telemetry.gpu.power` event needs.

  Candidate experiment: the pod sampler's first run on each pod (read-only). List the `power1_*` files in the card's hwmon directory and record 300 one-second idle readings. Expected movement: only `power1_average` present, integer watts, idle mean 38 to 40 W. Minimum sample size: 300 readings, which pins the idle mean to about plus or minus 0.2 W even with autocorrelated samples.

- **[practitioner]** PS-03. The upstream driver reports `gpu_metrics->energy_accumulator` on SMU 14.0.3 only when the power-management firmware is 0x00685000 or newer (commit 9413959fa9fe, "report energy accumulator for smu 14.0.3", committed 2026-09-17), after an earlier fix marked it invalid on SMU 14.0.2 because "EnergyAccumulator is unsupported on SMU 14.0.2" (commit ee193c5bbd5e, committed 2026-06-03, tagged for stable); the kernel's ASIC table lists the "AMD Radeon RX 9070 (XT)" as GC 12.0.1 with MP1 14.0.3, and LLVM maps gfx1201 to the Radeon RX 9070, 9070 XT and 9070 GRE. Citation: Linux commits https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/9413959fa9fe2d94d4814f8cc2b60409f4cd46b5 [S06] and https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/ee193c5bbd5e2b56bbeb54ef554414b43a6fc896 [S07]; Linux kernel documentation, Documentation/gpu/amdgpu/dgpu-asic-info-table.csv, https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/Documentation/gpu/amdgpu/dgpu-asic-info-table.csv [S08]; LLVM, "User Guide for AMDGPU Backend", processors table, https://llvm.org/docs/AMDGPUUsage.html [S09]

  Implies for our metrics: our pods report gfx1201 (our lab README, [S41]), so they should run SMU 14.0.3, but the energy counter, the better primitive (PS-06), exists only if the pod's amdgpu driver carries this September 2026 change and the card's power firmware is new enough; until checked, every pod energy number must record `source = avg_power_integral` or `source = energy_counter` (rubric test 1: every power number states its source).

  Candidate experiment: in the PS-01 slot on pod B, record `amd-smi version`, `amd-smi static --driver` and `amd-smi firmware`, then read `amd-smi metric --energy` twice, 5 s apart, during a steady GEMM load. Expected movement: either the energy field is N/A (no counter on this driver or firmware) or its 5 s delta is within 10% of 5 s x the mean `socket_w` (much tighter than Zeus's 0.1 to 10 ratio check in PS-08). Minimum sample size: 5 paired reads under load.

- **[practitioner]** PS-04. `amdsmi_get_energy_count` is documented for platform "gpu_bm_linux" with the note "It is not supported on virtual machine guest"; it returns an accumulator, the counter resolution in microjoules, and a timestamp with "Resolution: 1 ns", and "accumulates all energy consumed". Citation: AMD, AMD SMI library reference, Power Queries, `amdsmi_get_energy_count`, ROCm documentation 7.2.3, https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/amdsmi/en/docs-7.2.3/doxygen/docBin/html/group__tagPowerQuery.html [S02]

  Implies for our metrics: if the AUP node that hosts a pod is a virtual machine, the counter is unavailable regardless of driver and firmware; where it works, the counter's own timestamp should bound energy windows instead of the sampler's wall clock, which removes the sampler's read jitter from the attribution error.

  Candidate experiment: in the PS-01 slot, read the counter 1,000 times at 10 ms and check that the timestamp advances and the accumulator never decreases. Expected movement: monotonic accumulator, timestamp steps of 1 ms or more. Minimum sample size: 1,000 reads.

- **[practitioner]** PS-05. AMD SMI's environment variable `AMDSMI_GPU_METRICS_CACHE_MS` "Controls the internal GPU metrics cache duration (ms). Default 100, set to 0 to disable."; the CLI's `--watch INTERVAL` repeats a command every INTERVAL seconds, and `metric --power` is "Current power usage". Citation: AMD, "Using AMD SMI Command Line Interface tool", ROCm documentation (latest, read 2026-10-10), environment variables and `metric` sections, https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/amdsmi/en/latest/how-to/using-AMD-SMI-CLI-tool.html [S03]

  Implies for our metrics: a sampler polling AMD SMI faster than every 100 ms gets repeated cached values, which inflate samples per attributed window without adding information; the sampler should count only distinct updates in that metric, and since each CLI call starts a process and the CLI interval is in seconds, sub-second sampling should use the Python library or sysfs.

  Candidate experiment: in the PS-01 slot, poll 1,000 times at 10 ms with the cache at its default and 1,000 times with it at 0, under steady load. Expected movement: about 90% repeated values at the default; with 0, the distinct-update period shows the firmware's own refresh, unknown for RDNA4 (1 ms on MI250X, PS-06). Minimum sample size: 1,000 reads per arm.

- **[measured]** PS-06. On Frontier (512 MI250X GPUs) and Portage (480 MI300A APUs), square-wave tests showed that the rocm-smi average power on MI250X is firmware-filtered and "it takes a few seconds to fully capture the transition from idle to TDP", while MI300A's current power follows the transition in about 0.5 s; "The sensors refresh cumulative energy values at 1 ms granularity", power reconstructed by differencing the energy counter had "response and recovery times of a few milliseconds", and that derived on-chip power was "consistently lower than the Cray PM measurements by about 9% on Frontier across all benchmarks" (on-chip versus off-chip sensor placement); the averaging window of the average-power field "is not documented". Citation: McDaniel et al., "Fine-Grained Power and Energy Attribution on AMD GPU/APU-Based Exascale Nodes", 2026, arXiv preprint 2604.06056v2, Sec. II-B and V-A, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06056 [S30]

  Implies for our metrics: if the R9700's averaged field behaves like the MI250X's, a 5 s caption integrated from it can lose most of its first seconds and gain the previous request's tail, so the averaged field is unfit as the reference for the attribution-error metric while counter differencing (where live) is fit; on-chip numbers can sit about 9% below what a wall or board meter would show, and the slide must say "GPU socket" rather than "system".

  Candidate experiment: in the PS-01 slot, when the counter is live, difference it every 10 ms and compare each step's energy with the integral of `average_socket_power` over the same 10 square waves. Expected movement: the counter-derived power rises within 50 ms; the averaged field's integral over each 5 s on-period misses 10 to 40% of the step's energy (a 0.5 to 3 s lag on a 5 s step) and recovers it in the following off-period. Minimum sample size: 10 steps.

- **[measured]** PS-07. PowerSensor3 (up to 20 kHz sampling) found that "the built-in energy measurements of the W7700 GPU closely match PowerSensor3", and comparing it with both ROCm SMI and AMD SMI "yields identical results despite differences in their programming interfaces"; the Radeon Pro W7700 is an RDNA 3 card on the Navi 32 die (190 W board power). Citation: van der Vlugt et al., "PowerSensor3: A Fast and Accurate Open Source Power Measurement Tool", 2025, arXiv preprint 2504.17883, Sec. V-A1 and Fig. 7b, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17883 [S29]; M. Chiappetta, "AMD Radeon Pro W7700 Review", HotHardware, 2023-11-13, https://hothardware.com/reviews/amd-radeon-pro-w7700-review [S12]

  Implies for our metrics: one generation before ours, AMD's on-board energy figure was trustworthy against an external meter, which is evidence (not proof) that the R9700's counter, once live, can be our reference reading for attribution error; the `rocm-smi --showpower` fallback should agree with `amd-smi` exactly rather than act as a second opinion.

  Candidate experiment: the pod sampler's first run: read `rocm-smi --showpower` and `amd-smi metric --power` back to back 100 times under steady load. Expected movement: identical to the watt in at least 95% of pairs. Minimum sample size: 100 pairs.

- **[practitioner]** PS-08. Zeus supports AMD GPUs through AMD SMI from ROCm 6.3 only, because older versions returned wrong power and energy values; at start-up it reads the energy counter twice 0.5 s apart and trusts it only if measured over expected energy lies between 0.1 and 10 (code comment: "Loose bound to rule out very obvious counter problems"); when `current_socket_power` reads "N/A" it uses `average_socket_power`, and its AMD energy is `energy_accumulator x counter_resolution`. Citation: ML.ENERGY, Zeus documentation, "Measuring Energy", Hardware Support, AMD GPU, https://ml.energy/zeus/measure/ [S18]; Zeus API reference, `zeus.device.gpu.amd`, https://ml.energy/zeus/reference/device/gpu/amd/ [S19]

  Implies for our metrics: Zeus would run on the pods' ROCm 7.13 as a ready-made window sampler for benchmark rows, but its counter check passes a 10x error, so our own check (PS-03) must be tighter, and a Zeus number on the R9700 can silently be an integral of the averaged field; every Zeus-derived row must record which API path Zeus took.

  Candidate experiment: pod B, in the benchmark harness slot: wrap 5 steady 30 s decode windows in `ZeusMonitor` and in our own `socket_w` integrator. Expected movement: within 3% when both use the same field. Minimum sample size: 5 paired windows (CV of the paired difference assumed 2%, d = 3%: 7.85 x (0.02/0.03)^2 = 3.5, floor 5).

- **[practitioner]** PS-09. On an AMD SMI issue, a commenter measuring for Zeus (GitHub user jaywonchung) found that the MI100's energy counter did not move at idle and barely moved under load, while "the `average_socket_power` field updates correctly and we can measure energy by sampling it repetitively and integrating over time"; the same script's counter worked on MI210 and MI250, and an AMD maintainer replied "I'm not seeing these issues on mi200, mi210 nor mi300X". Citation: GitHub ROCm/amdsmi issue #38, "Incorrect Energy Consumption Reported by amdsmi_get_energy_count() Method", 2024, https://github.com/ROCm/amdsmi/issues/38 [S10]

  Implies for our metrics: counter correctness is per ASIC, so the R9700 counter must be validated at idle and under load before it becomes the reference reading; integrating the averaged field is the established fallback and is what `pod.idle_watts` and per-answer energy use until then.

  Candidate experiment: extend PS-03's check to 10 windows of 5 s, 5 at idle and 5 under load. Expected movement: counter delta within 10% of the integrated `socket_w` in both states, or a counter that stalls at idle (the MI100 failure). Minimum sample size: 10 windows.

- **[anecdote]** PS-10. One user's multi-threaded Rust reader saw `amdsmi_get_energy_count` values go backwards between reads; the log shows `res=15.3` (microjoules per count) on that user's GPU, and the reporter later wrote that with ROCm 7.0.0 or 7.0.1 "The problem described above no longer occurs" (with a library compiled from source). Citation: GitHub ROCm/amdsmi issue #115, "Energy counter goes backward in some conditions (Rust interface)", 2025, https://github.com/ROCm/amdsmi/issues/115 [S11]

  Implies for our metrics: the pod sampler should read the counter from a single thread and reject negative deltas as errors; at 15.3 microjoules per count the counter resolution is far finer than any window we attribute, so resolution is not a source of attribution error.

  Candidate experiment: PS-04's 1,000-read check plus a second run with two concurrent reader threads. Expected movement: zero negative deltas on ROCm 7.13 in both runs. Minimum sample size: 1,000 reads per run.

- **[measured]** PS-11. On our AUP R9700 pods (ROCm 7.13, no root), `amd-smi metric --power` and `rocm-smi --showpower` both work, `SOCKET_POWER` is reported in watts without root, and idle power was about 38 to 40 W; no sampling rate, duration, field or model state was recorded with the idle figure. Citation: our measurements, T3 task page "Pod GPU power sampler (amd-smi)" Done when and scope note, https://app.notion.com/p/3f596ef5fec5816da5e7c992c3f5b7e7, and the O1 telemetry rubric task page ("pod idle measured today at 38 to 40 W") [S42]

  Implies for our metrics: `pod.idle_watts` has a first value, but under rubric test 1 it is low confidence until the sampler re-measures it with source, rate, window and model state recorded; whether "idle" means an empty pod or a pod with the teacher resident decides the baseline policy (attribution-and-baselines.md AT-05).

  Candidate experiment: the pod sampler's first run, in two states on the same pod within one hour: empty pod, and teacher loaded with no requests. Expected movement: the two differ by less than 10 W; the measurement decides which one becomes `pod.idle_watts`. Minimum sample size: 300 one-second readings per state.

- **[measured]** PS-12. On pod A, vLLM serving `RedHatAI/Qwen3.8-27B-INT4` (W4A16 on the Triton fallback kernel, 16k context, one request at a time) decoded at 7.7 to 7.8 tok/s on text and 6.6 tok/s on a 720p image caption (TTFT 0.55 s text, 1.09 s image), held 84% of VRAM, and drew "~200 W average, 300 W peak, while generating"; the R9700's listed TGP is 300 W. Citation: our measurement, PR #37 body and lab/README.md on branch ctx/runtime/r2-gpu-lab (commit 32268ae), https://github.com/raohemdutt/Knighthacks26/pull/37 [S41]; Puget Systems parts listing, "AMD Radeon AI PRO R9700 32GB", https://www.pugetsystems.com/parts/Video-Card/AMD-Radeon-AI-PRO-R9700-32GB-16908/ [S13]

  Implies for our metrics: the teacher's prefill touches the board limit, so `teacher.watts_peak` = 300 W describes the limit more than the workload, and the 200 W `teacher.watts_avg` has no stated window or sampler and is low confidence; at batch 1 it is about 26 J per output token gross (attribution-and-baselines.md AT-20), the number batching should move.

  Candidate experiment: pod A through its sampler once it exists (requests only, no reservation): 20 captions of the 720p test image, one at a time, sampled at the fastest distinct-update rate (PS-05). Expected movement: decode-phase mean 180 to 220 W and prefill peaks at 300 W, with the throttle status reporting power limiting during the peaks if the card is capped there. Minimum sample size: 20 requests (per-answer CV assumed 10%, which gives the mean to about plus or minus 5%).

## 2. The Orin Nano through tegrastats and the INA3221

### Takeaway

The Orin Nano's power sensor is a three-channel INA3221 at I2C address 0x40: VDD_IN is total module input, VDD_CPU_GPU_CV the CPU, GPU and accelerator rail, VDD_SOC the SoC rail (PS-14); it does not see the carrier board (PS-16). tegrastats prints each rail as current/average mW once per second by default (PS-13), and the hwmon driver underneath averages a configurable number of conversions per reading (PS-15). On our exact board, polling the sysfs nodes every 15 to 20 ms and calibrating against a Monsoon monitor gave under 2.5% error on windows longer than 100 ms, while polling faster disturbed llama.cpp (PS-18). On older Jetsons the built-in readings lagged the load by about 2 s, so integrating only over the kernel's own window misattributed energy (PS-17). Our student answers take a few seconds (PS-20), so tegrastats' default gives only a handful of samples per answer; the Jetson sampler needs an explicit faster interval, a recorded averaging setting, and a lag check.

### Cited findings

- **[practitioner]** PS-13. tegrastats' `--interval <int>` is "The interval at which `tegrastats` is to write output to the log, in milliseconds. The default interval is 1000 msec."; each power field is printed as `VDDX Y/Z`, where Y is "Block's current power consumption in milliwatts" and Z "Block's average power consumption in milliwatts" (the averaging window of Z is not documented). Citation: NVIDIA, Jetson Linux Developer Guide r36.4, "Tegrastats Utility", command line options and power field output, https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/archives/r36.4/DeveloperGuide/AT/JetsonLinuxDevelopmentTools/TegrastatsUtility.html [S14]

  Implies for our metrics: at the default interval a 3 to 6 s student answer gets 3 to 6 samples, too few for a confident joules per answer (section 3); the parser must take Y (current) and integrate it, never Z, and the sampler must set `--interval` explicitly and record it with every sample.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, read-only, with Ilan's go: run tegrastats at 1000 ms and at 100 ms side by side with a 20 ms sysfs reader through 10 student answers. Expected movement: at 100 ms tegrastats energy per answer within 5% of the 20 ms sysfs integral; at 1000 ms within 15%, with the largest errors on the shortest answers. Minimum sample size: 10 answers, paired across samplers.

- **[practitioner]** PS-14. On Orin NX and Orin Nano modules, a three-channel INA3221 at I2C address 0x40 measures VDD_IN ("Total Module Power"), VDD_CPU_GPU_CV (CPU, GPU and CV cores including DLA and PVA) and VDD_SOC (the SoC core supplying the memory subsystem and engines such as NVDEC, NVENC, VI, VIC and ISP); voltage and current are read from `/sys/bus/i2c/drivers/ina3221/1-0040/hwmon/hwmon<x>/in<i>_input` and `curr<j>_input`, and overcurrent events are counted in `oc*_event_cnt`. Citation: NVIDIA, Jetson Linux Developer Guide r36.5, "Platform Power and Performance: Jetson Orin Nano Series, Jetson Orin NX Series and Jetson AGX Orin Series", Software-Based Power Consumption Modeling and Overcurrent Event Status, https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/archives/r36.5/DeveloperGuide/SD/PlatformPowerAndPerformance/JetsonOrinNanoSeriesJetsonOrinNxSeriesAndJetsonAgxOrinSeries.html [S15]

  Implies for our metrics: joules per answer on the box should integrate VDD_IN (the whole module), with VDD_CPU_GPU_CV kept as the model's share; channel power is voltage x current (our arithmetic, the guide gives the two reads but no formula); the overcurrent counters belong in `telemetry.jetson.power` so throttled windows can be flagged (power-and-clock-levers.md LV-10).

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, with Ilan's go: log all three rails at 20 ms through 10 student answers and 3 idle minutes. Expected movement: VDD_CPU_GPU_CV carries more than half of VDD_IN's rise over idle during decode, and VDD_SOC rises with memory traffic. Minimum sample size: 10 answers plus 3 idle minutes.

- **[practitioner]** PS-15. The Linux INA3221 hwmon driver exposes `samples`, the "Number of samples using in the averaging mode" (1, 4, 16, 64, 128, 256, 512 or 1024), and `update_interval`, the "Data conversion time in millisecond", following update_interval = C x S x (BC + SC) for C enabled channels, S samples and the bus and shunt conversion times; writing 0 sets both conversion times to the 140 us minimum. Citation: Linux kernel documentation, "Kernel driver ina3221", sysfs entries, https://docs.kernel.org/hwmon/ina3221.html [S16]

  Implies for our metrics: every VDD_IN reading is already an average over S conversions on the enabled channels, so the sampler must record `samples` and `update_interval` with each run (rubric test 1 asks for each number's sampling rate and error), and polling faster than `update_interval` only re-reads the same value and inflates samples per attributed window.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, read-only: read `samples` and `update_interval` for the INA3221 hwmon node in each nvpmodel mode, then poll `curr1_input` every 1 ms for 10 s and count distinct values per second. Expected movement: the distinct-update period equals `update_interval` within 10%. Minimum sample size: 10,000 reads.

- **[measured]** PS-16. Measuring a Jetson AGX Orin developer kit with PowerSensor3 on its USB-C supply, the authors found that "the time resolution of the built-in sensor is very limited (~0.1 second)" and that "the built-in sensor only measures the power consumption of the Jetson module", not the carrier board. Citation: van der Vlugt et al., "PowerSensor3: A Fast and Accurate Open Source Power Measurement Tool", 2025, arXiv preprint 2504.17883, Sec. V-B, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17883 [S29]

  Implies for our metrics: VDD_IN is module power, so the box's wall power (carrier board, cameras, USB, fan) is higher and must not be called "the box's power" on the slide or the stats page; at about 0.1 s resolution a 3 s answer still gets about 30 samples if the sampler polls at that rate.

  Candidate experiment: needs an inline USB-C or barrel-jack meter (hardware, Ilan's call): 10 answers measured at the supply and on VDD_IN at the same time. Expected movement: supply energy exceeds VDD_IN energy by a roughly constant carrier-board offset of a few watts. Minimum sample size: 10 answers.

- **[measured]** PS-17. On a Jetson TX2 (and an AGX Xavier for validation), power read manually from the onboard INA3221 sysfs nodes (polling loop resolution about 422 us, readings in steps of about 75 mW) took about 2.5 s to reach a kernel's peak (about 2 s on Xavier) and decayed for a while after it ended, so "the integral from t1 to t2 does not correspond to true energy consumption due to the delay and the tail in power profile"; a derivative correction with C = 415 ms plus a 9-point moving average recovered square profiles, and over windows that include the tail the raw and corrected energies differed by only 0.04% (42.449 J vs 42.467 J) and 0.17%. Citation: B. Aslan and A. Yilmazer-Metin, "A Study on Power and Energy Measurement of NVIDIA Jetson Embedded GPUs Using Built-in Sensor", 2022, 7th International Conference on Computer Science and Engineering (UBMK), IEEE, pp. 479-484, DOI 10.1109/UBMK55850.2022.9919522, https://web.itu.edu.tr/yilmazerayse/docs/Busra.pdf [S31]

  Implies for our metrics: on older Jetsons a per-answer window that stops at the answer's end leaks energy into the next request or into idle, while energy over long windows is robust; before joules per answer is trusted on the Orin Nano, its lag must be measured, and if it is comparable the request window must be extended past `ended` by the measured tail.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, with Ilan's go: 10 square-wave steps (5 s of a CUDA busy loop, 5 s idle) with VDD_IN polled at the INA3221 `update_interval`. Expected movement: the Orin's 10-to-90% rise time is far below the TX2's 2.5 s, on the order of the 0.1 s resolution in PS-16. Minimum sample size: 10 steps.

- **[measured]** PS-18. On a Jetson Orin Nano Developer Kit 8 GB (Jetson Linux 36.3), power was read from sysfs with perf and a custom script, "Measurements were recorded every 10 ms using perf" (an effective 15 to 20 ms interval on the Orin), calibrated by linear regression against a Monsoon High Voltage Power Monitor, giving a mean absolute percentage error "below 2.5% for both devices" for mean power over periods longer than 100 ms; shorter sleep intervals "caused significant interference with llama.cpp, distorting the results". Citation: M. Abstreiter, S. Tarkoma and R. Morabito, "Sometimes Painful but Certainly Promising: Feasibility and Trade-offs of Language Model Inference at the Edge", 2025, arXiv preprint 2503.09114v2 (under review), Sec. 4 and 6.3, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09114 [S38]

  Implies for our metrics: on our exact board a 15 to 20 ms sysfs sampler is accurate enough (under 2.5% after calibration) and cheap enough, while faster polling costs the student throughput, which bounds the sampler-overhead metric; the 2.5% depended on calibration against an external monitor, so our uncalibrated VDD_IN numbers need a stated error band.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, with Ilan's go: student decode tok/s on 16 identical prompts with no sampler, tegrastats at 1000 ms, tegrastats at 100 ms, sysfs at 20 ms and sysfs at 5 ms. Expected movement: under 1% tok/s loss down to 20 ms, a measurable loss at 5 ms. Minimum sample size: 16 answers per arm (decode tok/s CV assumed 2%, d = 2%).

- **[practitioner]** PS-19. Zeus measures NVIDIA Jetson energy, returning `soc_energy` with on-chip CPU, on-chip GPU and total energy in millijoules; metrics a board does not expose are returned as None, and non-Jetson hosts skip it. Citation: ML.ENERGY, Zeus documentation, "Measuring Energy", Jetson Platforms, https://ml.energy/zeus/measure/ [S18]

  Implies for our metrics: Zeus is a candidate off-the-shelf Jetson window sampler that would give the same window API on the box and on the pods (rubric test 4: the same definitions across nodes), but which rails its "total" sums must be checked against VDD_IN before its numbers are compared with ours.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, with Ilan's go: 10 student answers measured by Zeus and by our VDD_IN integrator at the same time. Expected movement: Zeus total within 5% of VDD_IN energy if it reads the same rail; a large gap identifies a different rail. Minimum sample size: 10 paired answers.

- **[measured]** PS-20. On our Orin Nano, the student (`Qwen3.5-4B` Q4_K_M plus `mmproj-F16`, llama-server) loads in about 40 s and runs text decode at about 15.7 tok/s and prompt processing at about 91 tok/s; no power was measured. Citation: our measurement, PR #36 "R8: atlas-llm service for the on-device Qwen3.5-4B" (merged as bf482f4) and its Build Log entry, https://github.com/raohemdutt/Knighthacks26/pull/36 [S43]

  Implies for our metrics: a 50 to 100 token answer decodes in 3 to 6 s, which gives 3 to 6 samples at tegrastats' default; joules per answer for the student does not exist yet, so the routing claim that the student is cheaper per answer is unmeasured until the Jetson sampler runs.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, with Ilan's go: the 5 golden questions, 4 repeats each, VDD_IN at 20 ms. Expected movement: tens of joules to about 100 J per answer (module power assumed, not measured, at 10 to 20 W, times 3 to 8 s), roughly an order of magnitude below the teacher's ~1.1 kJ per caption (attribution-and-baselines.md AT-20). Minimum sample size: 20 answers (CV 10%, mean to about plus or minus 5%).

## 3. Sampling error, window length and repeat counts

### Takeaway

The best-documented sensor study (more than 70 NVIDIA GPUs) found power readings that cover a quarter of runtime, average options that take a second to respond, and naive energy errors up to 70% that good practice cut to about 5% (PS-21, PS-22). Windows under 2 s are noisy even with energy counters, and the GPU's starting temperature biases the result (PS-23). Polling costs a CPU core and timing must be taken where the work happens (PS-24). Short prefill windows hold too few samples (PS-25), and MLPerf loops any workload to 60 s (PS-26). For us this means: per-answer energy on a 1 to 5 s answer integrated from a 1 s averaged field is inherently low confidence, steady windows of 5 s and longer are where numbers can be trusted, and samples per attributed window is the right guard. A source-backed threshold is a window of at least 5 s and at least 10 distinct samples for "high confidence", everything else flagged.

### Cited findings

- **[measured]** PS-21. Profiling more than 70 NVIDIA GPUs with micro-benchmarks and an external power meter, the authors found that on A100 and H100 "nvidia-smi only reports the average of the past 25ms every 100ms", so "only 25% of the runtime is sampled"; update periods ranged from about 20 ms (V100) to about 100 ms (A100), the "average" options showed "a linear growth over 1 second", the naive method's energy error "highly varies up to 70%", and their good practice cut the average error from 39.27% to 4.89%. Citation: Z. Yang, K. Adamek and W. Armour, "Part-time Power Measurements: nvidia-smi's Lack of Attention", 2024, arXiv preprint 2312.02741v3, Sec. 4 and 5, https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02741 [S28]

  Implies for our metrics: a vendor power field can be both averaged and partially sampled, and only comparison with a reference reveals it; the attribution-error metric needs exactly that reference (an energy counter or an external meter), and our first R9700 and Orin numbers should be published with an error band, not as exact values.

  Candidate experiment: pod B, in the PS-01 slot: fixed GEMM bursts of 50 ms, 200 ms and 1 s, 32 times each, integrating `average_socket_power`, compared with the counter (PS-03) if live. Expected movement: the error is largest for the shortest bursts (tens of percent) and smallest for the 1 s bursts. Minimum sample size: 32 repetitions per burst length (the study's own iteration count).

- **[practitioner]** PS-22. The same study's recipe: run the workload for 32 consecutive iterations or at least 5 s (inserting 8 evenly spaced delays when the averaging window is small), perform four separate trials with a randomized delay between them, and in post-processing discard repetitions that fall within the sensor's rise time and align the data with GPU activity. Citation: Z. Yang, K. Adamek and W. Armour, "Part-time Power Measurements: nvidia-smi's Lack of Attention", 2024, arXiv preprint 2312.02741v3, Sec. 5.1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02741 [S28]

  Implies for our metrics: benchmark rows (the harness's tok/s and J/token, the slide's numbers) should each be a steady window of 5 s or more, repeated in 4 trials with random start delays and with the first rise time discarded; per-answer numbers on short answers cannot meet this and must carry the low-confidence flag.

  Candidate experiment: pod B, benchmark harness slot: apply the recipe to the teacher's batch-1 J/token and report the CV across the 4 trials. Expected movement: CV under 5%, the residual error the recipe achieved. Minimum sample size: 4 trials (the recipe's count), extended to 8 if the CV exceeds 5%.

- **[measured]** PS-23. On 8 A100 GPUs running the attention layer of Llama 3.2 3B, 10 trials per configuration, "average energy consumption strongly correlates with the GPU temperature at the start of measurement"; without cooldown each trial inherits a hotter GPU, producing "biased and high-variance energy measurements"; "5 seconds of cooldown is enough for the GPU to return to a stable thermal baseline before each trial"; "Energy measurement stabilizes from 5 seconds onward", windows shorter than 2 s varied widely, and NVML's energy updates about every 100 ms. Citation: ML.ENERGY blog, "Thermally Stable Profiling for Accurate GPU Energy Measurement", 2026-03-09, https://ml.energy/blog/energy/measurement/thermally-stable-profiling-for-accurate-gpu-energy-measurement/ [S22]

  Implies for our metrics: per-answer energy measured back to back on a busy teacher drifts with temperature, so `telemetry.request` (or the matching power sample) should carry the GPU temperature at window start, and before/after arms must be interleaved or separated by a cooldown; 5 s is a defensible minimum window for a high-confidence number.

  Candidate experiment: pod B: 10 trials of a 5 s decode window with 5 s of cooldown between trials against 10 trials back to back. Expected movement: back-to-back mean energy higher by a few percent with a larger spread. Minimum sample size: 10 trials per arm (as in the source).

- **[practitioner]** PS-24. ML.ENERGY's best practices: prefer the energy API (read before and after, subtract) over a thread that polls power and integrates it, since polling occupies a CPU core and "will consume some amount of extra energy purely for energy monitoring"; synchronize the GPU before reading a window's end, because asynchronous dispatch makes naive timing "likely an underestimation of GPU computation time". Citation: ML.ENERGY blog, "Measuring GPU Energy: Best Practices", 2023-07-24, https://ml.energy/blog/energy/measurement/measuring-gpu-energy-best-practices/ [S21]

  Implies for our metrics: where a counter exists, the sampler should read it at window boundaries instead of polling; window boundaries must be stamped on the node doing the work, because a request timed on the Jetson includes the hub-proxy round trip, which at ~200 W adds energy that belongs to no answer.

  Candidate experiment: pod A through its sampler: compare Jetson-side start and end times with pod-side times for 50 teacher requests. Expected movement: the Jetson-side window is longer by tens to hundreds of milliseconds per request (at 200 W, 100 ms is 20 J, about 2% of a caption). Minimum sample size: 50 requests.

- **[measured]** PS-25. Hydra fused tegrastats and NVML telemetry with per-prompt timing on AGX Xavier, AGX Orin and AGX Thor (13 LLMs, about 107K per-prompt records); because tegrastats "samples at a fixed wall-clock interval", "short prefill windows often contain only a few samples, while longer decode windows are sampled much more densely", which explains the wider prefill variance, and the authors list this telemetry sampling asymmetry as a main threat to validity. Citation: A. Taherin et al., "Hydra: Phase-Aware Workload Characterization of LLM Inference across Edge SoC Generations, Backends, and Quantization Levels", 2026, IEEE International Symposium on Workload Characterization (IISWC 2026, accepted), arXiv 2608.25053, Sec. III and VI, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25053 [S39]

  Implies for our metrics: samples per attributed window should be stored per phase (prefill and decode), not only per request, and prefill energy on the Jetson (and on the pods' averaged field) is the least trustworthy part of joules per answer.

  Candidate experiment: fixture on this machine: replay recorded tegrastats traces at 1000 ms and 100 ms against recorded student request windows and count samples per prefill and decode window. Expected movement: at 1000 ms most student prefill windows (under 1 s at ~91 tok/s for prompts under 90 tokens) hold 0 or 1 samples; at 100 ms they hold 5 or more. Minimum sample size: 50 recorded windows.

- **[practitioner]** PS-26. MLPerf Power measures full-system power with SPEC-approved analyzers, rejects component-only measurement, and requires that "If a workload finishes executing before the 60-second mark, it is run in a loop until this threshold is reached", so that temporary fluctuations do not dominate the reported power. Citation: A. Tschand et al., "MLPerf Power: Benchmarking the Energy Efficiency of Machine Learning Systems from Microwatts to Megawatts for Sustainable AI", 2024 (revised 2025), arXiv preprint 2410.12032, Sec. III-C and IV, https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12032 [S27]

  Implies for our metrics: our numbers are component numbers (GPU socket on the pods, module VDD_IN on the Jetson) and must be labeled that way; when a number is meant to stand beside published efficiency figures, the workload should be looped to 60 s.

  Candidate experiment: pod B: teacher J/token from one 60 s looped window against the mean of twelve 5 s windows. Expected movement: within 3% once the engine is warm. Minimum sample size: 3 repeats of each design.

## 4. What the samplers cost

### Takeaway

Measured sampler costs exist only for other stacks: DCGM at 100 ms raised server power 5 to 10 W and disturbed performance (PS-27), AMD SMI reads stretch to milliseconds (PS-28), and sub-10 ms sysfs polling disturbed llama.cpp on an Orin Nano (PS-18). Window-style measurement costs two cheap reads (PS-29). The rubric wants the overhead measured and bounded; on the Jetson it matters most, because the student, the detector and the sampler share one module with a 25 W budget.

### Cited findings

- **[measured]** PS-27. On DGX A100 servers, "enabling DCGM consistently increases the IPMI server power usage by about 5-10W", and DCGM "may cause significant degradation and variability under certain configurations", so the authors took performance numbers from separate runs without DCGM profiling (DCGM at a 100 ms interval). Citation: P. Patel et al., "Characterizing Power Management Opportunities for LLMs in the Cloud", 2024, ASPLOS '24, DOI 10.1145/3620666.3651329, Sec. 3.4, https://foci.uw.edu/papers/asplos24-llmpower.pdf [S36]

  Implies for our metrics: the sampler-overhead metric needs both parts the rubric names, added power and lost throughput; on the Jetson's 25 W module a sampler costing even 0.5 W is a 2% bias in joules per answer, so the overhead must be measured on the box rather than assumed.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, with Ilan's go: VDD_IN with only a slow 5 s reader against VDD_IN with the production sampler at 20 ms, alternating 5-minute idle periods. Expected movement: added module power under 0.3 W at 20 ms. Minimum sample size: 3 alternating periods per arm.

- **[measured]** PS-28. On Frontier and Portage, sampling tool overhead "can stretch reads to a few milliseconds, more so on MI300A, where overhead and smoothing push safe attribution toward tens of milliseconds", and tool sampling overhead added jitter to read intervals, especially for rocm-smi. Citation: McDaniel et al., "Fine-Grained Power and Energy Attribution on AMD GPU/APU-Based Exascale Nodes", 2026, arXiv preprint 2604.06056v2, Sec. V-A1 and VII, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06056 [S30]

  Implies for our metrics: on the pods the sampler cannot resolve anything shorter than tens of milliseconds, which is fine for request windows of seconds but rules out per-token energy by direct sampling; per-token numbers must be derived as window energy over tokens.

  Candidate experiment: pod B, in the PS-01 script: time 1,000 `amdsmi_get_power_info` library calls and 100 `amd-smi metric --power` CLI calls. Expected movement: library calls take a few milliseconds or less; CLI calls take tens to hundreds of milliseconds because each starts a process, which rules the CLI out for sub-second sampling. Minimum sample size: 1,000 library calls and 100 CLI calls.

- **[practitioner]** PS-29. Zeus documents that "Measuring power and energy is also very low overhead, typically taking less than 10 ms for each call", and its window API needs only a read at the start and at the end. Citation: ML.ENERGY, Zeus documentation, "Measuring Energy", https://ml.energy/zeus/measure/ [S18]

  Implies for our metrics: window-style measurement (two reads per request) costs nothing that matters at our request lengths; continuous polling is where the overhead lives, so the overhead metric should be measured on the continuous sampler.

  Candidate experiment: pod B, in the PS-28 timing run: 1,000 `ZeusMonitor` begin and end pairs. Expected movement: each pair under 20 ms. Minimum sample size: 1,000 pairs.

## 5. RDNA4 firmware power fields, stale readings and counter units

Added 2026-10-10 by telemetry refinement pass 1.

### Takeaway

The RDNA4 power firmware keeps more power numbers than the driver hands out: its metrics table holds a moving-average and an average socket power, an average total board power, an energy accumulator and an update counter, while the upstream driver for SMU 14.0.2 and 14.0.3 exposes only the average socket power, and the accumulator only on new firmware (PS-30). So the pods read socket power, never board power, and nothing exposed counts the firmware's updates. A stale metrics read looks like a valid sample (PS-31), and the energy counter's units have differed between ASICs (PS-32), so neither a repeated reading nor a counter delta is trusted until the sampler has checked it on our card.

### Cited findings

- **[practitioner]** PS-30. In the upstream Linux headers for the SMU 14.0 power-management firmware (RDNA4), the metrics table `SmuMetrics_t` carries `MovingAverageSocketPower`, `EnergyAccumulator`, `AverageSocketPower` and `AverageTotalBoardPower` (lines 1681 to 1696) and a `MetricsCounter`; the SMU 14.0.2 and 14.0.3 driver copies `AverageSocketPower` into `gpu_metrics->average_socket_power` and into the average-power sensor (whole watts times `MILLIWATT_PER_WATT`), and `EnergyAccumulator` into `gpu_metrics->energy_accumulator` only when the MP1 version is 14.0.3 and the firmware is 0x00685000 or newer; that driver file reads neither `AverageTotalBoardPower`, `MovingAverageSocketPower` nor `MetricsCounter`. Citation: Linux kernel source at commit 3857c2f, drivers/gpu/drm/amd/pm/swsmu/inc/pmfw_if/smu14_driver_if_v14_0.h, `SmuMetrics_t` ([permalink](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/3857c2fe5449541e24afc5efdb0f81a8a8f9a3a0/drivers/gpu/drm/amd/pm/swsmu/inc/pmfw_if/smu14_driver_if_v14_0.h#L1649-L1727)) [S47]; drivers/gpu/drm/amd/pm/swsmu/smu14/smu_v14_0_2_ppt.c at the same commit, lines 701 and 2174 to 2176 ([permalink](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/3857c2fe5449541e24afc5efdb0f81a8a8f9a3a0/drivers/gpu/drm/amd/pm/swsmu/smu14/smu_v14_0_2_ppt.c#L2174-L2176)) [S48]

  Implies for our metrics: `socket_w` on the pods is socket (graphics package) power and cannot be read as board power, because the firmware tracks board power in a separate field the driver does not expose; every pod number, and the slide, says "socket", and the 300 W peak in `teacher.watts_peak` is a socket reading set against a board-level rating (PS-12). The firmware's update counter would separate fresh samples from repeated reads, but it is not exposed, so the sampler has to infer distinct updates from changing values and timestamps (PS-05), which is what samples per attributed window counts.

  Candidate experiment: in the PS-01 slot on pod B, print every `amd-smi metric` field once at idle and once under a steady GEMM load, and list which power and energy fields read a number and which read N/A. Expected movement: only the averaged socket power reads a number, plus the energy field if the pod's firmware is new enough; no board-power field appears. Minimum sample size: 2 full reads per state.

- **[practitioner]** PS-31. On four MI100 accelerators with ROCm 6.0.2, amdsmi's power-info and energy-count calls kept returning the same values, and `amd-smi metric -pE` printed the same `TOTAL_ENERGY_CONSUMPTION` (131.426 J) for all four GPUs; an AMD maintainer answered that the library did not force `gpu_metrics` to update, that commit 78074d7 fixed it, and that ROCm 6.1 carries the fix. Citation: GitHub ROCm/amdsmi issue #22, "Power info and energy counter APIs always return the same info", report of 2024-05-01 and maintainer reply of 2024-05-21, https://github.com/ROCm/amdsmi/issues/22#issuecomment-2122893234 [S46]

  Implies for our metrics: a stale read is indistinguishable from a real sample unless the sampler checks; our pods run ROCm 7.13, after the fix, but the pod sampler should still flag stretches of identical readings under load and count only distinct values toward samples per attributed window, so a stale source lowers that count instead of inflating it (rubric TT1).

  Candidate experiment: the pod sampler's first run on pod B: 60 s of reads every 100 ms under a steady GEMM load with `AMDSMI_GPU_METRICS_CACHE_MS=0`, counting stretches of identical readings. Expected movement: no stretch of identical readings longer than the distinct-update period the same run measures (PS-05); a longer stretch marks a stale read. Minimum sample size: 600 reads.

- **[anecdote]** PS-32. One user reports, on an issue that is still open, that with `amdsmi_get_energy_count` the power computed as resolution x 1000 x (accumulator delta) / (timestamp delta) matches the power the CLI reports on MI300A, MI300X and MI210, while on MI100 that formula is off by five orders of magnitude and 1e9 x (accumulator delta) / (timestamp delta) gives the right power instead, with a reported resolution of 15.3 on all of them; no maintainer has explained it. Citation: GitHub ROCm/amdsmi issue #38, comment of 2026-06-03, https://github.com/ROCm/amdsmi/issues/38#issuecomment-4612146012 [S10]

  Implies for our metrics: the counter's units are not uniform across ASICs, so on the R9700 the conversion from counter to joules must be checked against integrated socket power before any pod energy number comes from the counter (PS-03, PS-09); a counter that disagrees by a constant power of ten has a units error, not a sensor error.

  Candidate experiment: in the PS-03 check on pod B, convert each 5 s counter delta both ways (with and without the reported resolution) and compare each with 5 s x the mean `socket_w`. Expected movement: one conversion lands within 10% of the integrated power and the other is off by a constant factor, or both read N/A when the firmware is too old. Minimum sample size: 5 paired reads under load.
