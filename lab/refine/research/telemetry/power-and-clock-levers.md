# Power caps, clocks and power modes: what we can turn, and what it should do to joules per token

Track: telemetry. Opened and written 2026-10-10. Scope: what published measurements say about power caps, clock settings and Jetson power modes for LLM inference, which of those levers our nodes actually allow, and what each should do to joules per token and per answer. Companion notes: `power-sampling.md` (what the samplers report and their error) and `attribution-and-baselines.md` (windows, idle baseline, concurrent requests and batching). Every source is listed in `SOURCES.md` with its URL or DOI and the date it was opened; bracketed IDs such as [S35] point there.

How to read a finding. Each finding is one bullet. It opens with its label and an ID (LV-01 and so on), states what was found with its numbers and setup, and ends with its citation. Under it, "Implies for our metrics" says what it means for our metric set, and "Candidate experiment" names the node, the procedure, the expected movement and the minimum sample size. A pass cites a finding by file and ID, for example `power-and-clock-levers.md LV-05`.

Labels. `measured`: a number someone measured, with the setup stated. `practitioner`: a documented statement or recommendation from the people who build or run the tool (vendor documentation, driver source, maintainer replies, benchmark methodology). `anecdote`: a single user's report. `opinion`: argument without measurement.

Our metric set (the telemetry rubric and the Telemetry context spec): joules per answer per node, idle watts per node, sampler overhead (CPU percent and GPU interference), samples per attributed window, and attribution error against a reference reading; in the metrics file these appear as `teacher.watts_avg`, `teacher.watts_peak`, `teacher.joules_per_token`, `pod.idle_watts` and so on. `telemetry.jetson.power` carries the `nvpmodel` mode with every sample, and `telemetry.gpu.power` carries `gfx_mhz`, `mem_mhz` and `throttled`.

Minimum sample sizes. Unpaired comparisons use n per arm = 15.7 x (CV / d)^2, rounded up and never below 5, where CV is the run-to-run coefficient of variation of the metric, d is the relative change to detect, and 15.7 = 2 x (1.96 + 0.84)^2 (two-sided alpha 0.05, power 0.8). Paired comparisons (the same prompts in both arms) use 7.85 x (CV / d)^2 pairs. Until we have measured our own CVs, per-answer energy is assumed to have CV = 10% and steady-state window energy CV = 5%. This is an assumption, not a finding: 5% is the acceptance band Vellaisamy et al. use between repeated windows [S32] and close to the 4.89% residual error Yang et al. report after good practice [S28]. The first run of any experiment below replaces the assumption with our measured CV. A 7% effect on per-answer energy at CV 10% needs 15.7 x (0.10/0.07)^2 = 32 answers per arm, which is why most Jetson experiments below use 32.

## 1. Caps and clocks on data-center GPUs, and why the pods cannot use them

### Takeaway

On NVIDIA data-center GPUs, lowering the power cap or the clock saves energy at a small throughput cost for inference: a 250 to 175 W cap saved about 23% energy for about 7% more time on A100s (LV-01); locking the clock about 100 MHz below maximum cost under 2% performance (LV-02); decode, being memory-bound, kept its latency at clocks that cut power about 20% (LV-03). Power caps are reactive and mostly clip prefill spikes, while clock locks lower power throughout (LV-02). None of this is available on our pods: AMD SMI and Zeus need root or `SYS_ADMIN` to set caps and clocks, and the pods have no root (LV-04). The levers we do have on the pods are batching and engine software (`attribution-and-baselines.md` section 4); caps and clocks are a Jetson experiment.

### Cited findings

- **[measured]** LV-01. Running LLaMA 65B on four A100 80GB GPUs (GSM8K, batch 64, output lengths 256 to 1024), capping each GPU from 250 W to 175 W raised inference time by 6.2 to 7.4% and cut energy by 21.8 to 24.0% ("an average of 6.7% for a corresponding average reduction in total energy by 23.21%"), with token rate down 5.9 to 6.9%; at 150 W, time rose 15.3 to 21.7%, energy fell 32.8 to 34.7% and token rate fell 13.2 to 17.8%. Citation: S. Samsi et al., "From Words to Watts: Benchmarking the Energy Costs of Large Language Model Inference", 2023, arXiv preprint 2310.03003, Sec. IV-E, Table III, https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03003 [S35]

  Implies for our metrics: if the pods ever allowed a cap, the teacher's ~200 W decode average under a 300 W limit (`power-sampling.md` PS-12) suggests a cap near 200 W would cost little decode time while clipping prefill spikes; today this informs only the Jetson mode choice and a possible request to AMD staff, not a pod experiment.

  Candidate experiment: not runnable on the pods without root. If AMD staff grant cap control for one session (Ilan's call: accounts and hardware), the teacher at caps of 300, 250 and 200 W, 5 steady windows each. Expected movement: at 200 W, J/token down 10 to 20% with decode tok/s down under 5%. Minimum sample size: 5 windows per arm (CV 5%, d 15%: n = 1.7, floor 5).

- **[measured]** LV-02. Characterizing LLM inference on DGX A100 servers, POLCA's authors found "less than a 2% drop in performance at ~100MHz (7%) below the maximum frequency" and that "significant power (up to 20%) can be reclaimed for minimal performance loss (up to 7%)" by frequency locking; power capping "is reactive and triggers when power exceeds a set threshold, making it more likely to only impact prompt phase power draw", whereas frequency locking "reduces overall power on demand with minimal performance loss". Citation: P. Patel et al., "Characterizing Power Management Opportunities for LLMs in the Cloud", 2024, ASPLOS '24, DOI 10.1145/3620666.3651329, Sec. 4.2 (Insight 7, Fig. 10), https://foci.uw.edu/papers/asplos24-llmpower.pdf [S36]

  Implies for our metrics: on the one node where we control clocks (the Jetson), a fixed clock somewhat below maximum is the first lever to try, and any such experiment must report both `watts_peak` and joules per answer, because caps and clock locks move them differently.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, with Ilan's go: MAXN_SUPER with clocks pinned at the mode's maximum against a custom mode whose GPU maximum is one available step lower, 32 golden-question answers each. Expected movement: J/token down a few percent with decode tok/s down under 2%. Minimum sample size: 32 answers per arm (CV 10%, d 7%).

- **[measured]** LV-03. Sweeping H100 frequency from 800 to 1980 MHz for Llama-2 70B on vLLM (8 GPUs, tensor parallel), Stojkovic et al. found the decode phase memory-bound with time between tokens barely affected by frequency, about 20% lower power for most configurations "without any impact to the latency or throughput", about the same throughput at 1.6 GHz instead of 2 GHz at "less than 80% of the energy", and up to 15% energy saved by lowering the maximum batch size during low-load phases. Citation: J. Stojkovic, E. Choukse, C. Zhang, I. Goiri and J. Torrellas, "Towards Greener LLMs: Bringing Energy-Efficiency to the Forefront of LLM Inference", 2024, arXiv preprint 2403.20306, Sec. IV, https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20306 [S37]

  Implies for our metrics: the student's decode on the Jetson is bandwidth-bound too (102 GB/s), so lowering the GPU clock should cut J/token without hurting tok/s, while lowering the memory clock should hurt both (LV-08); the batch-reduction result says nothing useful for a teacher that already sits at batch 1.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, with Ilan's go: the 25W mode (GPU 918 MHz, memory 3199 MHz) against a custom mode with GPU 612 MHz and memory still at 3199 MHz, 32 student answers each. Expected movement: decode tok/s within 5%, J/token down 10 to 20%. Minimum sample size: 32 answers per arm.

- **[practitioner]** LV-04. Zeus's AMD backend calls `_warn_sys_admin()` before every power-cap, clock-range or clock-reset call, which need the Linux `SYS_ADMIN` capability ("virtually `sudo`"), while "Reads stay local to AMD SMI (they are unprivileged)"; in containers Zeus needs `--cap-add SYS_ADMIN` to change power limits or frequency. AMD SMI's CLI marks `set` and `reset` as requiring "sudo" privileges and states no such requirement for `metric`. Citation: Zeus API reference, `zeus.device.gpu.amd`, https://ml.energy/zeus/reference/device/gpu/amd/ [S19]; Zeus documentation, "Getting Started", System privileges and Docker, https://ml.energy/zeus/getting_started/ [S20]; AMD, "Using AMD SMI Command Line Interface tool", ROCm documentation, https://rocm.docs.amd.com/projects/amdsmi/en/latest/how-to/using-AMD-SMI-CLI-tool.html [S03]

  Implies for our metrics: on our pods (no root, per our lab README [S41]) power reads work and power writes cannot, so no routing policy or experiment may assume pod caps or clock locks; `telemetry.gpu.power.throttled` and the clocks are our only view of the firmware's own power management on the pods.

  Candidate experiment: the pod sampler's first run on each pod: read the power limit without root (`amd-smi static --limit`) and log `throttled`, `gfx_mhz` and `socket_w` through 10 teacher captions. Expected movement: limit 300 W, readable without root; the clock dips while power sits at the limit during prefill. Minimum sample size: 1 limit read per pod and 10 captions.

## 2. Jetson Orin power modes

### Takeaway

The Jetson is the only node where we can change power modes and clocks. NVIDIA's Super modes raise clocks and published LLM throughput by 1.3 to 1.6x but publish no energy figures (LV-05, LV-06). On our exact board, before Super modes existed, the 15 W mode used up to 7% less energy per generated token than the maximum-clock mode and up to 25% less in prefill (LV-07); on an AGX Orin, cutting the GPU clock to 800 MHz lowered energy, while 400 MHz or a lower memory clock raised it (LV-08). The likely sweet spot is a middle GPU clock with the full memory clock, which is roughly what the 25W mode is; MAXN_SUPER buys speed with joules and can throttle (LV-05, LV-10). Q4_K_M, the student's format, had the lowest decode energy per token among llama.cpp formats on AGX Orin (LV-09).

### Cited findings

- **[practitioner]** LV-05. For the Orin Nano 8GB with the Super configuration, NVIDIA lists 15W (mode ID 0: CPU max 1497.6 MHz, GPU max 612 MHz, memory max 2133 MHz), 25W (mode ID 1, the default: 1344, 918 and 3199 MHz) and MAXN_SUPER (mode ID 2: 1728, 1020 and 3199 MHz), the last described as "an experimental mode" available only with the `jetson-orin-nano-devkit-super` flash configurations; MAXN "does not guarantee the best performance for all use cases" because hardware throttling engages when total module power exceeds the TDP budget. Citation: NVIDIA, Jetson Linux Developer Guide r36.5, "Platform Power and Performance: Jetson Orin Nano Series, Jetson Orin NX Series and Jetson AGX Orin Series", Supported Modes and Power Efficiency, https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/archives/r36.5/DeveloperGuide/SD/PlatformPowerAndPerformance/JetsonOrinNanoSeriesJetsonOrinNxSeriesAndJetsonAgxOrinSeries.html [S15]

  Implies for our metrics: the student's mode is a recorded condition of every Jetson number (`telemetry.jetson.power.nvpmodel`); the 15W mode also cuts the memory clock by a third (3199 to 2133 MHz), which for a bandwidth-bound decoder should cut tok/s by up to a third, so 15W is unlikely to win on J/token for our student even though it won in LV-07's older setup.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, with Ilan's go: 15W, 25W and MAXN_SUPER, 32 golden-question answers each, with VDD_IN at 20 ms. Expected movement: tok/s about 30% lower at 15W than at 25W and about 10% higher at MAXN_SUPER; J/token lowest at 25W, within 10% of the others. Minimum sample size: 32 answers per mode (CV 10%, d 7%).

- **[measured]** LV-06. NVIDIA's JetPack 6.2 announcement reports LLM generation on the Orin Nano 8GB, INT4 through the MLC API, before and after Super mode: Llama 3.1 8B 14.00 to 19.10 tok/s (1.37x), Llama 3.2 3B 27.70 to 43.10 (1.55x), Qwen 2.5 7B 14.20 to 21.80 (1.53x), Gemma 2 2B 21.5 to 35.0 (1.63x), Gemma 2 9B 7.20 to 9.20 (1.28x) and Phi-3.5 3.8B 24.70 to 38.10 (1.54x), with GPU clock 625 to 1020 MHz and memory bandwidth 68 to 102 GB/s; it gives no energy or power figures. Citation: NVIDIA Technical Blog, "NVIDIA JetPack 6.2 Brings Super Mode to NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano and Jetson Orin NX Modules", 2025-01-16, Table 4, https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-jetpack-6-2-brings-super-mode-to-nvidia-jetson-orin-nano-and-jetson-orin-nx-modules [S17]

  Implies for our metrics: the speedups (1.28 to 1.63x) sit closer to the bandwidth gain (1.5x) than to the GPU clock gain (1.63x), consistent with bandwidth-bound decode (our reading of the table); the vendor publishes no joules per token for Super mode, so ours would be the first for this student in this mode, and our measured 15.7 tok/s (`power-sampling.md` PS-20) must be stored with the mode it ran in.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, read-only: record the current `nvpmodel` mode, then re-run 10 answers to compare with the published 3.8B-class rate. Expected movement: our llama.cpp student below MLC's 38.1 tok/s for the 3.8B model in Super mode (different engine and a 4B vision model). Minimum sample size: 10 answers.

- **[measured]** LV-07. On a Jetson Orin Nano Developer Kit 8 GB (Jetson Linux 36.3, llama.cpp, sysfs power calibrated against a Monsoon monitor), "the 15W mode consumed up to 7% less energy per token than the MAX mode during generation phase", and MAX (CPU and GPU clocks at their maximum within the 15W mode) used up to 25% more energy per token in prefill for models up to Yi 1.5 6B. Citation: M. Abstreiter, S. Tarkoma and R. Morabito, "Sometimes Painful but Certainly Promising: Feasibility and Trade-offs of Language Model Inference at the Edge", 2025, arXiv preprint 2503.09114v2 (under review), Sec. 5.3, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09114 [S38]

  Implies for our metrics: maximum clocks cost energy per token on our board even before Super mode existed, so the energy-optimal mode for the student is probably not MAXN_SUPER, and the answer may differ between prefill-heavy requests (captions) and decode-heavy ones (chat), so the mode experiment must report both phases.

  Candidate experiment: inside LV-05's run, split energy into prefill and decode using llama-server's per-request timings. Expected movement: MAXN_SUPER worst on prefill J/token by 10 to 25%. Minimum sample size: 32 answers per mode.

- **[measured]** LV-08. On a Jetson AGX Orin 64GB (JetPack 6.0, Hugging Face Transformers, power from jtop), a custom mode with the GPU at 800 MHz cut power by about 28% and raised latency by about 26% for lower total energy than MaxN; the GPU at 400 MHz cut power by 51% but raised energy; the memory clock at 665 MHz raised latency by 370% and energy by 72%; fewer CPU cores had a negligible latency effect. Citation: M. Arya and Y. Simmhan, "Understanding the Performance and Power of LLM Inferencing on Edge Accelerators", 2025, PAISE 2025 (IPDPS workshop), arXiv 2506.09554v2, Sec. 3.4, Table 2, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09554 [S40]

  Implies for our metrics: J/token over GPU clock has a minimum in the middle, the memory clock should never be lowered for decode, and parking CPU cores is a way to cut idle watts per node without slowing the student.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, with Ilan's go: custom modes at three available GPU steps from 612 to 918 MHz with memory fixed at 3199 MHz and the CPU fixed, 32 answers each. Expected movement: J/token minimum at an intermediate step, 5 to 15% below the 918 MHz value, with tok/s down under 10%. Minimum sample size: 32 answers per mode.

- **[measured]** LV-09. Hydra measured llama.cpp decode energy on an AGX Orin: for Qwen2.5-3B, Q4_K_M used 903 mJ per token against 1,209 for Q8_0 and 1,227 for Q6_K; for Qwen2.5-7B, 1,867 against 2,462 and 2,559; power did not follow bit width (for 3B, Q6_K drew 48.5 W against 39.0 W for Q8_0 and 45.0 W for Q4_K_M). Citation: A. Taherin et al., "Hydra: Phase-Aware Workload Characterization of LLM Inference across Edge SoC Generations, Backends, and Quantization Levels", 2026, IISWC 2026 (accepted), arXiv 2608.25053, Sec. V-C, Table VI, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25053 [S39]

  Implies for our metrics: the student's Q4_K_M is already the energy-cheapest llama.cpp format, so re-quantizing is not a joules lever (it may still be a quality lever), and power alone cannot rank formats, one more reason the stats page needs joules beside watts.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson with the app stopped (memory, and Ilan's go): Qwen3.5-4B in Q4_K_M against Q8_0, 32 answers each. Expected movement: Q4_K_M 20 to 30% lower J/token, as in the source's 3B and 7B rows. Minimum sample size: 32 answers per arm.

- **[practitioner]** LV-10. The Orin series reports three kinds of overcurrent events (under voltage, average overcurrent and instantaneous overcurrent), shows which are enabled in `oc*_throt_en` and counts them in `oc*_event_cnt`; when total module power exceeds the mode's budget the module throttles, which is why MAXN "does not guarantee the best performance for all use cases". Citation: NVIDIA, Jetson Linux Developer Guide r36.5, "Platform Power and Performance: Jetson Orin Nano Series, Jetson Orin NX Series and Jetson AGX Orin Series", Overcurrent Event Status and Supported Modes and Power Efficiency, https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/archives/r36.5/DeveloperGuide/SD/PlatformPowerAndPerformance/JetsonOrinNanoSeriesJetsonOrinNxSeriesAndJetsonAgxOrinSeries.html [S15]

  Implies for our metrics: every Jetson power window should record the overcurrent counters before and after; an increase means the window ran throttled, its J/token belongs to a different operating point, and it should carry the low-confidence flag.

  Candidate experiment: Jetson, with Ilan's go: the student plus the detector at full load for 10 minutes in 25W and in MAXN_SUPER, reading `oc*_event_cnt` every 10 s. Expected movement: no events at 25W; some events possible in MAXN_SUPER under the combined load. Minimum sample size: 3 ten-minute runs per mode.
